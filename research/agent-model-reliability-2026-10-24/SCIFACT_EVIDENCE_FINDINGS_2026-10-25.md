## Experimental provenance and scientific meaning

The original released [SciFact paper (Wadden et al., EMNLP 2020)](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/) provides scientific claims with human-annotated SUPPORT or CONTRADICT evidence papers and exact rationale sentence indices. The source archive has 5,183 paper abstracts, 300 development claims, and SHA-256 `11c621288d41ac144d29b13b0f8503b3820b7d6e8b1f6ff24dff335c196d76be`. Its development annotations contain **188 claims with known evidence**, **209 distinct expert-annotated claim/document pairs** (138 SUPPORT, 71 CONTRADICT), and **338 original rationale alternatives**. Other development claims with no annotated document are not automatically irrelevant. The original expert annotation schema and correct disjunctive-rationale-set scoring come from the project's [data](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/data.md) and [evaluation](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/evaluation.md) manuals. Expert rationale sentence sets are alternatives, not simply separate independently valid sentences; missing one sentence in a multi-sentence rationale means the complete rationale has **not** been retrieved.

The method was frozen in `SCIFACT_OFFLINE_EVIDENCE_BASELINES_2026-10-25.md`, with the source-checking, single-thread CPU implementation committed in `evaluate_scifact_offline_v1.py` *before execution*. It compares BM25 and TF-IDF over the complete unlabeled source corpus for paper retrieval; for sentence ranking inside **known expert-annotated papers** it compares BM25, TF-IDF, exact lexical overlap, first sentences, last sentences and fixed-seed random selection. No source claim-evidence label was provided to an algorithm. No LLM, new model, GPU, data-cleaning-dependent label optimization or training was used. Nitro was running other CPU-intensive production, so this experiment used one CPU thread with low priority. The original scientific text is retained outside Git in private Nitro scratch, not copied to Thor.

Reproducible, committed derived evidence without full copyrighted source text: `evidence/scifact-offline/source_evidence_metrics_v1.json`, `evidence/scifact-offline/source_evidence_case_predictions_v1.json.gz` (every source ID, original expert rationale index, exact predicted top-five sentence IDs and condition result; decompression SHA-256 `231b87d23620ba8560f036e2c0e8740d6b2f7bd4b153fbbe5c48c68256171e06`), and the explicitly **exploratory post-hoc** cascade file `evidence/scifact-offline/exploratory_cascade_v1.json`. The private 3-megabyte source tar and full original derived JSON remain at `/data/tmp/agent-model-independent-qrels-20261025`. ## Measured source-document retrieval across all 5,183 papers

Among the 188 development claims with one or more known annotated evidence papers, TF-IDF placed at least one **known** annotated paper at rank one in **62.77%**, at rank five **82.45%**, and within rank ten **89.89%**. BM25 achieved **70.74%**, **87.77%** and **94.15%** at the same budgets. At ten retrieved papers, the proportion of annotated claims with at least one correct original evidence paper was therefore **177 of 188** for BM25. These are observed *known-evidence hit rates* against the available SciFact labels; the source contains no exhaustive judgments of the remaining unannotated papers, so document-level precision and false-positive rates are not established. The claim wording was derived from source literature, which may make exact lexical retrieval unusually effective. This is a valid baseline, not proof of optimal real-web research retrieval.

## Measured complete rationale preservation at a three-sentence context budget

On the 209 expert-annotated claim/document pairs, with the correct paper already supplied, the fraction for which the top-three selected original sentences included **at least one complete gold rationale set** was:

* first three sentences **22.01%**;
* last three sentences **66.99%**;
* deterministic random three **49.28%**;
* simple lexical overlap **74.16%**;
* TF-IDF word uni/bigram **80.86%**;
* BM25 unigram **80.38%**.

The TF-IDF/first-three paired difference was **58.85 percentage points**, with a descriptive claim-clustered bootstrap interval **51.78–65.84 points** from 1,000 resamples of 188 source claims, not 209 incorrectly independent pairs. The BM25/first-three difference was **58.37 points** (bootstrap interval **50.98–65.53**). BM25 and TF-IDF were essentially tied at three sentences: BM25 minus TF-IDF **negative 0.48 percentage points**, descriptive interval **negative 3.50 to positive 2.44 points**. This does not justify declaring one ranker superior by training-set selection. TF-IDF retained **39.39%** of the average original sentence characters while finding a complete expert rationale in about **81%** of the cases. A text budget is not a semantic fidelity guarantee, and shorter is useful only when the correct original relationships survive.

The positional baseline is unusually informative. Gold rationale sentence indices tended toward the **latter portions** of the original abstracts (mean normalized evidence position roughly **0.65**). Last-three selection's high **66.99%** hit rate is a dataset positional bias, not evidence that source recency reliably makes information more important. This is a direct warning against conflating position and semantics when benchmarking input context selection; the original owner's requirement to preserve important historical or nonrecent constraints remains valid.

## Negative evidence: incomplete conjunctions and correlated selector errors

Of the 209 expert-annotated claim/document pairs, **14** had no valid single-sentence rationale alternative: a correct answer required at least two source sentences together. TF-IDF retrieved a complete rationale in only **four** of those fourteen within three sentences; BM25 did so in only **five**. The entire-corpus 81%-success summary therefore hides a sharp failure on multi-premise reasoning. In original claim `295`, paper `20310709`, the annotated alternative rationales were sentence sets `[2,4]` **or** `[5,6]`. TF-IDF's selected IDs `[2,1,6]` included fragments of both sets but **neither complete alternative**; counting any matched sentence as successful would have manufactured a pass. A second original claim `443`, paper `10165258`, required `[4,5,6]`; both lexical methods selected `[0,3,6]` and missed the necessary conjunction. No original scientific text or gold labels were rewritten to obtain this result.

On the same 209 source-grounded cases, TF-IDF and BM25 at a three-sentence limit were **both correct 164 times**, **both wrong 36 times**, with only **five TF-IDF-only successes and four BM25-only successes**. The paired binary-success phi correlation was **0.86229**. This is an observed correlation of **lexical evidence retrieval methods**, *not a correlation of LLM reviewer mistakes*. It demonstrates that two apparently different selectors can share most missing-evidence failures, consistent with the original recording's warning that voting or agreement is not proof. A combined lexical selector would need to be evaluated against the same independent source labels; model ensembles remain unmeasured here.

## Exploratory error cascade, independently recomputed from frozen predictions

The preregistered stages were separately measured; an additional source-linked exploratory analysis joined the already frozen rankings **after observing the separate outcomes**. The script `analyze_scifact_cascade_v1.py` does not rerun or modify any original source prediction. When BM25 retrieves the top **ten** documents and TF-IDF selects the top **three** sentences of each annotated candidate, an entire expert rationale remains available for only **148 of the 188** annotated claims, or **78.72%**. BM25 had already retrieved some known evidence document for **177** claims. That means **29 additional claims** lost their complete expert-evidence opportunity at the sentence-selection stage, even after a correct evidence document entered the context. BM25-for-both-stages produced **147 of 188**, or **78.19%**. The contrast isolates a *necessary access-to-evidence* bottleneck in a retrieval cascade; it does not quantify a downstream Qwen factual-answer loss or a net gain from an extra LLM call. Source labels outside the known evidence corpus remain unknown.

## New independent research on syntactic auxiliary training

A direct primary study closer to the user's originally requested auxiliary-language-modeling question is [Du et al., *Exploiting Syntactic Structure for Better Language Modeling* (ACL 2020)](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.591/). It trained language models to predict both next words and parse-derived **syntactic distances**, reporting **lower heldout perplexity** on Penn Treebank and Chinese Treebank for its architecture. This is positive evidence for an actual *training-time auxiliary syntax objective*, not just prompts containing parse tags. [Sartran et al., *Transformer Grammars* (TACL 2022)](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.81/) found sentence-level perplexity and syntax-sensitive generalization gains but a **document-level perplexity penalty** associated with its restrictive recursive composition mechanism. [Lindemann et al., *Strengthening Structural Inductive Biases* (EMNLP 2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.645/) report improvements in few-shot syntactic tasks and structural semantic parsing after syntactic-transformation intermediate pretraining. In contrast [Glavaš and Vulić (EACL 2021)](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/) found inconsistent downstream semantic-language-understanding benefits from supervised syntax parsing, while [Hu et al. (ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.478/) report improved natural-language loss and generalization from a different intervention—hierarchical formal-language *pre-pretraining*.

The evidence therefore **answers a limited version of the original question affirmatively**: grammatical auxiliary training *can* improve heldout language-model outcomes in specific controlled experiments, but effects depend strongly on architecture, amount of data, objective, context length and outcome definition; no universal improvement or Qwen-specific benefit is established. These studies do not universally compare FLOPs, parameter counts, or training corpus sizes under identical contracts. The earlier 6,110-parameter synthetic GRU pilot is not adequate contrary evidence against benefits for actual transformer language models. No new local training was performed here.

## What remains objectively unanswered

The user's distinction between user-visible importance, worker research/action necessity and orchestration sequencing is supported by source/role semantics but has **not** been validated by blinded human ratings plus real worker execution effects on a large authentic agent-task distribution. On local Qwen, ordered textual versus integer scores still have only a few source-anchored pairs showing representation sensitivity in **both** directions, not a statistically reliable selection. A fair real-model comparison of full context versus *working automated* evidence-select-then-answer remains incomplete due to capacity interruptions; earlier source-selected savings are an oracle-bound, not specialist-call success. The SciFact private-data transfer to Thor was explicitly denied and **was not retried or rerouted**; no SciFact Qwen judge-model inference can be claimed. No sufficient cross-model matched wrong/wrong observations exist for a model-ensemble correlation estimate. Modern decoder-only transformer auxiliary syntax training against truly compute-matched controls remains unperformed locally. Do not infer answer correctness, tool-execution authority, quantization effects or reviewer independence from the new CPU retrieval figures.

**Practical interim conclusion:** keep exact primary sources and tool purposes durable, assemble task-specific evidence without conflating user-facing and worker-research relevance, test complete premise sets and source-authority constraints, and account for both document-stage and sentence-stage recall. Use machine-consumable relevance as prioritization metadata, not factual authority. The final success criterion remains independently verified **worker action or source-grounded answer correctness** under latency/resource constraints. Future tests require genuine free model slots and permitted independent-source access, not repetitions of denied actions or more trivial fixture counts.
