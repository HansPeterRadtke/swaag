# Original research question: does agreement/combining multiple selectors recover more independent evidence? Frozen before train-split evaluation, 25 October 2026

The October seventeenth/eighteenth owner recordings ask whether multiple models, perspectives and decomposed evidence selectors improve *actual information correctness*, not simply produce more plausible votes. The project has an already measured original SciFact **development-split** benchmark: BM25 and TF-IDF each recovered about eighty percent of complete expert rationale sets at a three-sentence budget, **both missed thirty-six** of 209 cases, and both correctly retrieved 164. Its source identifiers and raw predictions are preserved; none is overwritten or rerun here. That result motivates, but does not prove, ensemble advantage. This extension tests *independent heldout evidence selection* on the separately released **train** claim split without ever interpreting lexical agreement as verification of a scientific statement.

## Data, independence and privacy

Canonical original SciFact source is Wadden et al., EMNLP 2020, https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/ ; original release https://scifact.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/release/latest/data.tar.gz ; immutable archive SHA-256 `11c621288d41ac144d29b13b0f8503b3820b7d6e8b1f6ff24dff335c196d76be`. The source train split has eight hundred nine claims; the development split has three hundred claims. Source IDs do not overlap, but **two identical claim strings appear in both splits**. Exclude all train claims whose exact string also occurs in development before scoring. Do not inspect model answers or evaluation success from this heldout train set before fixing algorithms. The train split contains five hundred five evidence-labeled claims and five hundred sixty-four claim/document evidence pairs before exclusions; hundreds of original alternative expert rationale sets are preserved as OR-of-complete-sentence-sets. Neither paper text nor original claims are committed to Git; scripts, original source hash, source IDs, gold sentence indices, rank positions and derived metrics are shareable in the owning SWAAG research branch subject to license precautions.

## Fixed comparable procedures

The already committed `evaluate_scifact_offline_v1.py` supplies frozen lexical primitives: `TfidfVectorizer` lowercased word uni/bigram, L2 norm with sublinear TF; word-unigram BM25 with k1=1.2 and b=.75, Robertson-positive IDF; and stable source-index order for ties. Reuse without changing these hyperparameters. Fit document rankers to the entire *unlabeled* original 5,183-paper corpus. Rank all candidate sentences of each independently annotated paper using only the claim and the source sentences; the expert rationale labels are *only scoring oracles*. Test these methods:

- direct TF-IDF; direct BM25; first sentences; last sentences; seeded uniform random;
- rank fusion of BM25 and TF-IDF sentence ranks with fixed reciprocal-rank-fusion constant 60, both weights one;
- alternating one unique sentence from BM25 then one from TF-IDF in a round-robin stream, then repeating BM25, preserving source-order tie breaks;
- RRF with TF-IDF and reverse-position ranking (constant 60, weights one); RRF with BM25 and reverse-position ranking; and three-way RRF with TF-IDF, BM25 and reverse-position ranking (all equal weights and constant 60). Positional combinations are **explicitly development-informed candidate methods**, not neutral predev theory. If they fail heldout, preserve that negative result.

At a fixed source-context sentence budget K=1,2,3,4,5, measure the fraction of independently annotated **claim/document pairs** where **at least one entire valid expert rationale alternative** is included (not mere one-sentence overlap). Stratify by label SUPPORT vs CONTRADICT, rationale length, and original abstract length. In addition show original source character fraction retained for selected K. No model answer correctness or true entailment is inferred from rationale coverage alone.

For document retrieval, compare original single TF-IDF vs BM25 vs equal-weight reciprocal-rank-fusion across all 5,183 source papers, with fixed constant 60. Report presence of any known expert evidence document at rank 1/5/10/20/50, not false-positive precision because missing qrels are not known negatives. Combine fixed document top-ten retrieval with selected sentence top-three to measure a **necessary source-evidence availability cascade** for an expert claim. For later contrast, report analogous fixed document top-one and top-five budgets. A document and full expert rationale must *both* be present. Separately report empirically observed cofailure and correlation of TF-IDF vs BM25, and whether rank-fusion can improve at identical three-sentence source budget or only by increasing that budget.

All hyperparameters, seed `20261025`, budget settings and reports are fixed before the new train-split run. A **claim-clustered** one-thousand-resample bootstrap estimates differences in three-sentence full-rationale coverage between the single and fused rankers, with source claim as sampling unit; preserve paired denominators, return baseline miss counts and estimated confidence bounds. This is an *out-of-development distribution transfer*, not a prospectively registered paper-wide randomized trial: two split claim strings overlap and are excluded, paper documents overlap, source itself is public and may exist in model training data. Preselecting a method based on results of this exact test would consume this split; do not tune and then claim the same outcomes are fresh heldout generalization.
