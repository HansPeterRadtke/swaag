## Answer 1: context required for user-facing answers versus agent work

**Verdict: task- and consumer-conditioned, source-exact relevance is the correct criterion, not permanent 'interestingness' or a global relevance percentage.** The worker needs operative source facts and prohibitions, exact original user goal and complete causal/tool observations; the orchestrator needs action prerequisites, live state, model routes, worker status and receipts; the communication model needs the specific information the user requested, verifiable current outcome, important blocker/uncertainty and applicable user preference. It need not read raw debugger telemetry to tell an ordinary user 'not finished', but must surface internal details when the user specifically asks for them. Original tool output needs originating tool request and source/revision/time authority; a quote of an old user instruction must not override a later explicit correction. Cached relevance scores are navigation hints, not proof of truth or evergreen authority.

The existing **source-authentic SWAAG relevance corpus** contains 64 researcher-labeled role/purpose/source judgments grounded in 29 distinct exact project-report excerpts. **Eleven original excerpts** receive opposite necessity labels (0 versus 8) in different explicitly specified task contexts. That is a concrete representation of the owner's user-facing-versus-worker distinction but **not independent human adjudication**. External source evidence strengthens a prerequisite for any such filtering: on original SciFact annotated claims, full-corpus BM25 found a known expert evidence document in top ten for **93.5% of 505 source claims**; at three selected sentences, BM25 preserved a whole expert rationale in **80.9% of 564 known claim/paper cases**, and a document-top10/sentence-top3 cascade preserved some complete rationale for **77.0% of 505 claims**. There are critical multi-premise failures, with a three-signal selector at K3 only recovering **10 of 32** claim/paper examples requiring more than one sentence. These are CPU retrieval results against independent expert annotations, **not** measured end-to-end SWAAG worker/action outcomes or Qwen reasoning correctness. Raw release and derived metrics stay preserved in `SCIFACT_EVIDENCE_FINDINGS_2026-10-25.md` and `OVERNIGHT_4_FUSION_AND_QWEN_2026-10-25.md`.

**Conditional implementation winner:** exact sources, cheap deterministic/lexical indexed retrieval and complete evidence-set preservation whenever applicable; avoid an unnecessary preliminary semantic model call if the relevant exact material already fits. On two complete Qwen Coder *same-question* pairs, delivering researcher-curated two-source evidence in one answer call preserved correct short answers and reduced total tokens by **45.0%** and **56.4%** compared with direct all-five-source calls, with only modest wall-clock savings. Those are **oracle-curated context upper bounds**, not proof an automated LLM selector works. [Liu et al., *Lost in the Middle* (TACL 2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/) independently shows the dependence of contextual extraction on evidence position, even within supported long-context windows. A context that fits is not a guarantee the model interprets its authority correctly: one full-source Coder call incorrectly preferred an old quote to a later authoritative user correction, although the source explicitly denied that priority.

## Answer 2: numeric relevance versus ordered textual categories

**Verdict: no proven universal format winner; use a machine-consumable ordinal format with an explicit purpose, and test actual semantic decisions.** Both constrained 0–8 integers and mapped nine-level text enums can encode the same order. They are neither calibrated probabilities nor interchangeable with *distinct purpose dimensions* such as user-facing value, work quality and orchestration priority. The real choice is what makes each installed model consistently judge necessity for the original task and preserve the final answer's required evidence; both format and criteria wording affect the result.

The original fully frozen authentic SWAAG dataset has **13 completed local Qwen judgments** in Thor's append-only journal, an increase from the previously committed eleven. The source journal was inspected **read-only**; its SHA-256 at this checkpoint is `fb4bfa476ce30ee693e0d6cbef0d51752b00d4f3b6241a7554a4f1c7fe7c4437`, stored on Thor at `/data/tmp/agent-relevance-corrective-20261024/observations.jsonl`. No request was repeated. **Five genuinely matched same-model/same-source/same-role numeric/category pairs** exist. Under the **researcher-assigned extreme-label** exact criterion, both encodings matched the declared anchor in **three of five** pairs. With a threshold of six for 'required', numeric agreed in **four of five**, textual in **three of five**. Numeric total absolute ordinal error on these five pairs was **10** versus textual **16**. Numeric dominated two Coder pairs, text dominated one Qwen3.8 pair, and two ties occurred. These differences **are not statistically reliable and the 'gold' relevance anchors were not independently human-adjudicated**; the exact +2 or +8 deviations are not automatically a real-world mistake if the final user/worker outcome remains correct. No production scoring format should be reconfigured solely from these data.

The strongest *independent primary-literature alternative* is not 'pick ordinal words rather than numbers'. [Lee et al., *CheckEval* (EMNLP 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.796/) evaluated **twelve LLM judges**, finding strong human-judgment correlation and **0.45 greater average inter-model agreement** when broad subjective ratings were decomposed into explicit binary checklist attributes. This does **not** show that a textual enum is inherently superior to a numeric rating, or that the checklist requires twelve sequential model calls. [Lee et al., *Evaluating the Consistency of LLM Evaluators* (COLING 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.710/) directly documents self-consistency and cross-scale disagreement on various interval/Likert/binary formats. This favors validating explicit source-answer facets rather than relying on a single ungrounded 'relevance 90%' scale. Applying a facet checklist in **one** structured call for short foreground tasks is a practical hypothesis, not a measured SWAAG winner.

## Answer 3: fused call versus decomposed/specialist calls, accuracy and full cost

**Verdict: choose the smallest number of model calls that preserve the necessary evidence and complete the semantic decision correctly; decomposition is conditionally effective, not generally superior.** The earlier SWAAG **SmolVLM2-2.2B-Instruct Q4_K_M**, serving via llama.cpp with an 8,192-token context, had 198-plus retained isolated results for various schema/semantic responsibilities. In one concrete tool-call case, `tool_calls`-only succeeded **6/6** while a rich fused action failed **0/6**; but a separate abstract action-class stage also failed **0/6**, and two-tool selection remained faulty. On a communication-status test, the rich fused call got the principal status right **6/6**, while a stripped-down boolean `finished` call got it wrong **6/6**. Binary relevance and exact evidence-record selection often over-selected, and hand-simplifying *exact* completion evidence changed verdict-only success from **0/6 to 6/6**. This shows the *output shape and factual evidence representation* can each determine quality. These small task-specific cases do **not** demonstrate a universal end-to-end two-LLM-chain improvement, especially not on current Qwen.

For current **Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B Q4** and **Qwen3.8-27B Q8** on authentic SWAAG source questions, the frozen full-context direct-answer matrix has **five matched tasks** with both models finishing: Coder **4/5** versus Qwen3.8 **5/5**, with mean full-response latency **1.270 versus 4.544 seconds** (3.58 times longer for Qwen3.8 on those specific calls). Prompt lengths and hardware contention limit generalization; base models **and quantizations** differ, so this is not an isolated model-size or quantization experiment. The one cross-model disagreement is the historical-correction authority task, correct only on Qwen3.8. No conclusion that Qwen3.8 is uniformly more accurate or always worth its latency follows from five tasks.

**Measured specialist latency lower bound:** on the history-authority task, Coder's initial model-selected source-IDs stage cost **1,021 total tokens / 1.703 seconds**, already exceeding Coder's completed direct answer of **1,016 tokens / 1.549 seconds**. Qwen3.8's selector alone cost **1,030 tokens / 4.900 seconds**, essentially the cost of the completed direct answer **1,031 tokens / 4.951 seconds**. A second answer call necessarily incurs additional positive tokens and latency: on these observed tasks, selector-first cannot be *faster or cheaper in total* even if second-stage correctness later improves. The incomplete stages cannot be counted as final semantic errors or successes; they were preempted/refused by the production-idle admission gate. A separate Coder selector returned complete originally identified source IDs for another real tokenizer-authority task, but did not complete its second answer. This is **valid negative cost evidence**, without invented completed-chain accuracy.

[Select-Then-Decompose (Liu et al., EMNLP 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.278/) explicitly selects decomposition style based on task demands and achieves a reported quality/cost Pareto frontier **on its evaluated datasets**. [Decomposed Prompting (Khot et al.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02406) showed task-specific multi-hop and symbolic reasoning benefits. Neither proves that putting a standalone selector before SWAAG's first voice response improves quality or latency. The later Infra contract requires **one capable initial communication-model call**: retain it. Specialist work can proceed behind the first user-facing answer, with exact source provenance and full recorded token/GPU cost.

## Answer 4: model ensembles and reviewing correctness

**Verdict: other models can identify missed possibilities and genuine errors, but agreement alone never upgrades a conclusion to a verified fact.** Judge candidate outputs against independent original source passages, expected tool effects, runnable tests, formally valid premises, or outside labels. Preserve wrong/wrong cases, exact source/seed/prompt/model identifiers and uncertain premises; do not collapse correlated votes into a probabilistic 'proof'. The single observed matched Coder/Qwen3.8 disagreement on a source-grounded history-authorization question is a useful prompt for verified correction, not enough to estimate review accuracy or model-to-model error correlation.

On the independent expert-annotated SciFact retrieval benchmark, two distinct *lexical ranking algorithms* (BM25 and TF-IDF) simultaneously missed a complete expert rationale for **96/564** train claim/paper cases; their binary full-rationale hit/miss correlation was **0.786**. Pure lexical reciprocal rank fusion returned **450/564**, worse than BM25 alone's **456/564** at an identical three-sentence budget. Including differently biased sentence position increased full rationale coverage to **470/564**, a **+2.48 percentage point** descriptive improvement over BM25 with a claim-clustered interval including zero. **This is NOT cross-LLM review accuracy**. It is hard evidence for the general failure mode: different seeming methods can share almost all meaningful errors; diverse relevant evidence is more valuable than additional same-source votes.

[Kamoi et al., *When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?* (TACL 2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/) concludes that unsupervised prompted self-correction lacks broad reliable evidence while **external feedback** and task-specific verification can work. The original owner's proposed stronger-model intermittent reviewer remains a *resource- and ground-truth-conditioned hypothesis*; no arbitrary larger-model weight is scientifically justified. The correct performance measure is end-to-end verified action/answer improvements net of extra GPU and wall latency, not number of reviewers agreeing.

## Answer 5: grammatical and syntactic auxiliary training

**Verdict: YES, training-time syntax signals can improve held-out language modeling in particular architectures and corpora; NO, a universal improvement or one for currently installed Qwen has not been shown.** [Du et al., ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.591/) jointly trained next-word and parse-derived syntactic-distance objectives, observing lower perplexity on Penn and Chinese Treebanks compared with their evaluated baselines. [Sartran et al., *Transformer Grammars* (TACL 2022)](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.81/) found better sentence perplexity and syntax-sensitive generalization, with **worse document-level perplexity** due to a restrictive composition/memory bottleneck. [Glavaš and Vulić, EACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/) observed limited/inconsistent downstream semantic-understanding benefit from intermediate supervised syntax. Results depend on inductive bias, training data size, grammar annotation noise, length scale, model type, objective and compute. They demonstrate *possibility*, not same-FLOP universal dominance.

The prior local **6,110-parameter synthetic GRU** three-seed experiment had exactly 50% verb-number accuracy on all three equal-update arms and nearly equal language-model loss; it was too small to generalize to modern transformers. **No Qwen training** was attempted: Thor disk remains about 98% occupied, production workers share GPU, and matching initial weights/training FLOPs/data at that scale is a different research project. Applying grammar tags only in a prompt would not answer the user's actual auxiliary-training question.
