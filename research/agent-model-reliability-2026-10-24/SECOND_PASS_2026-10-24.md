## Training-level grammar-auxiliary experiment, distinct from prompting

A separately executed **CPU-only** tiny language-model training trial now exists under `evidence/grammar-toy/`. `grammar_aux_synthetic_experiment.py` is the *executed* reproducible source, `grammar_aux_synthetic_results_20261024.json` contains all nine completed results and the predefined disjoint lemma-pair train/validation/test splits, and `grammar_aux_run_failed_initial.txt` preserves the initial target-length mismatch as an invalid setup rather than a model failure. The training used existing PyTorch CPU, no package/model downloads, a single CPU thread, and did not contend with Nitro rendering or Thor's production models.

This is a genuine **training-time auxiliary-label** comparison rather than a syntactic prompt. A 6,110-parameter, single-layer GRU next-token language model with the same auxiliary head in all configurations was trained under exactly three different seeds and three treatments: plain LM loss, LM plus true generated grammatical role/number labels, and LM plus deterministically shuffled labels of the same overall frequency. Every run used the same 192 training sentences, thirty optimizer updates, 6,720 input tokens seen, five epochs, learning rate and network/parameter count. The true- and shuffled-label treatments used the same 0.3 auxiliary-loss weight and comparable operations; the plain-LM treatment computed the auxiliary head but weighted its loss zero. On sixty heldout new subject/distractor lemma-pair combinations, all three groups scored **0.50 subject–verb agreement-number accuracy in each of the three seeds**. Mean next-token cross-entropy: plain **1.29924**, true syntactic supervision **1.29920**, shuffled-label control **1.30955**. The apparent language-model loss difference between true syntax and plain baseline is around **0.00004**, effectively negligible; there is no reliably measurable syntactic accuracy improvement under this experiment's tiny data/training budget.

The synthetic grammar uses artificial fixed-length sentences and even mechanically regular plurals rather than unrestricted human language. It did **not** tune weight or epochs after looking at the heldout outcome and made no statistical superiority claim from three seeds; its equal-compute interpretation is especially strong for the two auxiliary-label arms, not a guarantee of exact floating-point-operation parity versus the zero-weight plain branch. The outcome does **not** answer whether syntactic auxiliary objectives improve a modern trained transformer, any installed Qwen weights, or natural-language generalization. Repeating this toy with more optimization after seeing its result would consume the evaluated heldout data; a new holdout is necessary for unbiased model selection.

## Independently verified published findings, separate from these observations

Primary paper sources were independently checked on the ACL Anthology on October twenty-fourth. Glavaš and Vulić, *Is Supervised Syntactic Parsing Beneficial for Language Understanding Tasks?* (EACL 2021, https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/), report **limited, inconsistent downstream semantic understanding gain** from intermediate syntactic parsing of pretrained transformers. Strubell and colleagues, *Linguistically-Informed Self-Attention for Semantic Role Labeling* (EMNLP 2018, https://aclanthology.org/D18-1548/), report **task-specific improvement** from jointly learned syntactic information in semantic-role labeling, not general decoder-only pretraining. Hu and colleagues, *Between Circuits and Chomsky* (ACL 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.478/), report **improved heldout natural-language loss/generalization** using hierarchically structured *formal-language pre-pretraining* in their selected transformer regime and a one-billion-parameter experiment; this does **not** directly test dependency-tag auxiliary supervision on installed Qwen. Taken together, the literature demonstrates plausible mechanisms and contradictory task-conditional outcomes, not an unconditional yes/no answer. Genuine compute-matched current-Qwen auxiliary training remains unperformed and would require separate weights and safe, authorized resources.

Existing sources for call decomposition, positional context sensitivity, quantization and ensembles remain in `REPORT.md` and Infra's research appendix. No new large-context ordering, model/quantization ablation, user-grounded relevance gold standard or sufficiently powered ensemble sample was run in the second pass. The earlier categorical-vs-numeric **two matched pairs** remain unchanged and too few to establish a preferred representation. Additional tests must use a larger external oracle and balance position/order, seeds and source use; a one-off valid JSON prediction is not full work-quality evidence.
