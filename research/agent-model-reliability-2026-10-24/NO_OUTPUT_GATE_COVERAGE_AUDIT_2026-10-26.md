# Independent no-output gate coverage audit — October 26, 2026

This audit uses the already-present matched original-source outcome analysis and does not rerun any previous model request, use an after-the-fact outcome as a deployable verifier, or alter live models. Source: `DECISIVE_MATCHED_COMPUTE_FINDINGS_2026-10-25.md`, section “Decisive evidence round four”; primary study archive and hashes recorded there. The source release contains 20 distinct task IDs × 7 exact model versions per benchmark (140 dependent model-task evaluations), with an external final task grader and no complete per-task inference latency/tokens.

For SWE-bench Verified, 63/140 direct single-agent runs succeeded; 77 failed, including only 14 with *no output*. No-output therefore observes 14/77 = **18.18%** of verified failures; **81.82% produced an output but were wrong**. Under the fixed centralized alternative, no-output gating rescued 7 tasks; independently verified-failure gating rescued 16. Thus the cheap no-output trigger accesses **7/16 = 43.75% of this alternative's observed rescue opportunities**, leaving 9/16 unaddressed.

For Terminal-Bench, 38/140 direct single-agent runs succeeded; 102 failed, including only 19 with no output. No-output covers 19/102 = **18.63%** of verified failures. Under the fixed independent alternative, no-output gating rescued 4; verified-failure gating rescued 15. Thus no-output accesses **4/15 = 26.67% of observed rescue opportunities**, leaving 11/15 unaddressed.

These fractions answer a narrower operational question than aggregate accuracy: **absence of output is an observable, high-specificity *failure event*, but is not an adequate comprehensive trigger for second-stage review**. When tests can independently establish failure, they surface the majority of missed retry opportunities. The oracle success-selection policy is not a real online verifier, so its extra successes cannot be claimed in live SWAAG; retrying a side-effecting workflow also requires receipt inspection and idempotence. These descriptive percentages are not cross-Qwen reviewer accuracy, and the denominator is not 140 independent task identities.
