## Original question 1 — Is relevance different for a user answer, worker work and orchestration?

**Yes; the authority/purpose distinction is grounded in the owner's requirements and actual SWAAG sources, but automated semantic performance is not independently proved.** User-facing turns need relevant current result, source-grounded uncertainty and requested detail; workers need exact operative requirements, prohibition scope, full tool inputs/outputs; orchestration needs exact worker/plan identifiers, dependencies, current revisions, receipts and whether effects actually occurred. The same source is not equally relevant in all three contexts. The existing 64 relevance annotations on 29 actual SWAAG report excerpts are **single-researcher labels**, not independent human correctness gold; eleven passages deliberately change label by task/role. `context_grounding_v1.json` also leaked required source IDs as universally `r1,r2`, so its high apparent selection quality is invalid as blinded semantic accuracy. The last real SWAAG scripted-foreground production-path experiment had **12/23** unsupported final claims under the committed guard versus **1/23** under an *undeployed* candidate, at **24 versus 37 mock-model requests**. The only complete final checked effects were two genuine local calculator receipts. That is *harness* evidence; no real Qwen user/worker semantic accuracy estimate was measured. Most extra delegations were safe but not task completion.

**Interim practice:** preserve full source revision, exact original user instructions and current-turn supersession; use task-role-scoped evidence metadata as prioritization, not semantic authority. If the user requests internal details they become user-facing evidence too. No automated single global relevance number can prove a tool effect.

## Original question 2 — Numeric scores or ordered textual categories?

**Unresolved on the actual installed models.** Five source-identical same-model Qwen trials completed both a 0–8 integer and a nine-level ordered-text judgment. Both matched single-researcher anchors in **3/5**; numeric absolute ordinal errors totaled **10**, textual **16**, but the labels were not independently human-adjudicated and the sample is tiny. Both formats can be sorted after explicit ascending mapping. A relevance rating is not a probability of truth, and an independent source/effect pass–fail predicate is not the same estimand as a subjective user-interest rating. No policy choice follows from the observed 3/5 tie. A real next evaluation needs multiple human-approved ratings/ranges, same task/role, actual final answer/worker results and source-preserving negatives.

**Task-conditional; no local Qwen decomposition winner.** Six genuine matched original Qwen user/worker source questions had **5,593 tokens / 20.874 seconds** for complete direct answers, versus **5,600 tokens / 21.827 seconds for selector *first stages alone***. The corresponding automated selector→answer second stages were not completed. The one-capable-model first user-facing call remains the original foreground contract; independent sizable background jobs may use separated workers when actual task dependencies and evidence warrant it. Neither the isolated synthetic full-service control tests nor an unrelated CPU lexical study establishes Qwen performance.

Original, already available independent agent-study source is Kim et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2026, Zenodo archive v2.1.3 SHA `3b6752af8271db7319b1b23dbbe93733bd13e585c87e6f1abc4a17e15b96dc35`, internal task grader record SHA `c7bde9eac1f28a29397c10ab10e29f0962595e9dd45f17aedc2d309289b12007`, original publisher figure source SHA `402a9fea7430bf4dbf1645c8bafd7850811ffb5bb331568e8088d85c4dc9ffd9`. The 1,800 records contain externally graded `resolved`, and `success` is uniformly zero and **not** a grader. The study includes 20 distinct original SWE-bench Verified tasks and 20 distinct Terminal-Bench tasks, repeated across **seven** exact original model versions and evaluated separately under five architectures; eligible case comparisons are **140 model/task units per benchmark**, *not 140 independent task IDs*. **No online oracle:** a terminal `no_output` flag and `num_steps` (reasoning step count) are available after primary execution. The original independently judged `resolved` and later alternative grades were hidden from all **deployable trigger selectors**, used only in scoring. `cross_benchmark_failure_risk_budget_20pct` fitted an unchanged two-feature logistic model `[no_output, log1p(num_steps)]` on original external grades of the *other* benchmark, and ranked exactly **28/140** cases (20%) in the target benchmark. The test checked that inverting every **target** gold success/failure and every alternative outcome left all eight nonoracle trigger policies **unchanged**; only the expressly labeled retrospective `verified_failure_oracle` changed. This guards against data-leakage mistakes, but is not a prospective untouched domain: the original source has been read in prior research. The policies were applied to the same 140 original source task/model units across **all four** alternative architectures; the new final result is the source grader's actual outcome of the alternate architecture in triggered cases, *blindly replacing* the primary outcome. A second architecture would be a **complete extra workflow**, not one model call. The original alternatives were independently executed by the study authors, *not as retries following the primary*, so these outcomes are an **offline conditional-routing counterfactual**, not a verified live replay-safe action.

### Observed completed-task outcomes on matched original cases

All entries are **independently graded correct final tasks out of 140**. The number in parentheses is additional complete alternative architecture workflows requested by that observable policy; the primary single-agent workload is required in every condition.

| Original benchmark / alternative | Primary alone | No primary output fallback | Source-blind hash 20% | Cross-domain risk 20% | Always rerun alternative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SWE-bench Verified, centralized | 63 (0) | **70 (14)** | 63 (28) | 68 (28) | 62 (140) |
| SWE-bench Verified, decentralized | 63 (0) | **68 (14)** | 62 (28) | 68 (28) | 59 (140) |
| Terminal-Bench, centralized | 38 (0) | **43 (19)** | 34 (28) | **44 (28)** | 31 (140) |
| Terminal-Bench, decentralized | 38 (0) | **44 (19)** | 36 (28) | **45 (28)** | 39 (140) |

The independent and hybrid alternatives, favorable and unfavorable, are **also retained in the full ledger**. This is descriptive model/task mean over seven repeated model versions, not necessarily the task mix of SWAAG. The simple observable no-output gate is *high precision, low recall*. On SWE-bench Verified it flags exactly **14 of 77 graded primary failures** and zero correct primaries (14/77 failure recall, 0 false-positive triggers); on Terminal-Bench **19 of 102** and zero correct primaries. Source outcomes thus support **no-output as a concrete terminal failure symptom, not as a comprehensive error detector**. A no-output occurrence does not establish that an external side effect is safe to retry without receipts, especially in a real SWAAG workflow.

The cross-benchmark risk trigger recognized more primary failures at the same 28-complete-workflow cap, but was not a guaranteed better recovery policy: **SWE-bench 23/28 triggers were actual failures, 5 originally successful; Terminal-Bench 27/28 failures, 1 originally successful**. For centralized fallback on SWE-bench, risk recovered **8** wrong primary results but broke **3** originally correct ones, net **+5 successes**, whereas `no_output` recovered **7** without breaking any at **half the additional workflows**. For centralized Terminal-Bench risk recovered **6**, broke **0**, net **+6**, versus no-output **5** recoveries with **nine fewer workflows**. For decentralized Terminal-Bench, risk recovered **7**, broke **0**, net **+7** versus no-output **6** at 19 workflows. Avoid equating “trigger predicts primary failure” with “the alternative is likely to solve it.” In these sources many failures are *shared* across architectures, so extra work can fail or destroy a correct primary result.

All-alternative unconditional fallback is an important negative control: on SWE-bench centralized it rescued **16** original failures but also broke **17** correct primaries, yielding **62/140 vs direct 63/140** despite 140 complete additional workflows. On Terminal-Bench centralized it rescued **14** but broke **21**, leaving **31/140 vs direct 38/140**. This is an actual original-grader observation, **not a hypothetical vote**. Hindsight switching on independently graded failed originals can only be shown separately as an unimplementable oracle upper bound; it cannot guide production switching without a reliable, authorized independent verifier. Simply rerunning another model because the first produced a surprising answer is not independently verified improvement.

### Task-clustered uncertainty and resource boundaries

Paired effect intervals use **1,000** reproducible bootstrap resamples of **20 original task IDs** in each benchmark, carrying *all seven model outcomes for each task* together. They are descriptive, not formal prospective confidence guarantees; they do not establish independent sevenfold task replication. Centralized risk20-minus-always-direct net correct outcome was **+5 of 140** SWE-bench model/task units, descriptive interval **−1.43 to +8.57 percentage points** (includes zero), and **+6 of 140** Terminal-Bench units, interval **+1.43 to +7.14 points**. SWE centralized risk20-minus-no-output was **−2 outcomes with 14 more workflows**, interval **−4.29 to +1.43 points**; Terminal centralized risk20-minus-no-output was **+1 outcome with 9 additional workflows**, interval **0 to +2.14 points**. The per-model task outcomes are **not independent** and the source/algorithm has been retrospectively inspected; do not treat these figures as validated deployable policy rankings.

Original archive `num_steps` is reported reasoning-workflow steps (can include zero for early exit), *not comparable GPU-seconds or total tokens*, particularly across coordinated architectures; it is used only as a failure-risk feature and descriptive supplemental column. Extra workflow count is accurately countable; real GPU queue wait, peak VRAM, total token usage, model server latency, restart/preemption cost, and independent effect receipts for **local** SWAAG/Qwen remain unavailable. **No new model was run, no original benchmark task was rerun and no production GPU or worker touched.** The trained failure detector on published results is a possible policy *research input*, not a ready-to-deploy general verifier. Original source `resolved` cannot be consulted online when deciding to review, and a blind fallback is not automatically safe when prior work may have side effects.

## Original question 4 — Does auxiliary grammar training help?

**Can help on particular architectures/benchmarks, but Qwen-specific and FLOP-matched efficacy remains unproved.** Earlier documented controlled published studies found both syntax-specific and generalization gains and document-level/some downstream regressions. The synthetic 6,110-parameter GRU pilot was not representative; prompting grammatical tags is not training-time auxiliary syntax. This round did not invent new Qwen training on a heavily shared/occupied GPU. A legitimate local result needs same original model/initial weights, compute budget and corpus with and without auxiliary syntactic loss, heldout language modeling plus actual agent-task outcomes, and training/inference resource accounting. The original question is not answered by the new escalation/source-routing test.

## Operational recommendation and next specific unproved requirement

Keep the one initial semantic foreground communication call, complete current user requirements and exact authoritative state, and independent source/tool receipts for consequential facts. A verifiably missing final output is a **specific high-confidence error symptom** that can warrant *inspection* or a carefully idempotent retry. Before any expensive actual second architecture/workflow is attempted, check that the first had no effects or its outcomes/receipts are reconciled; do not blindly reuse an independently executed alternative result as if a live second attempt would do the same. The new cross-benchmark step-based risk trigger is not strong enough to justify production rollout: it spent extra workflows to obtain small, model/task-correlated, sometimes negative additional gains. No universal winner exists across methods, models and task types.

The main unresolved empirical barrier remains **authentic owner SWAAG task turns on resident Qwen Coder and Qwen3.8 with genuinely completed specialist/reviewer stages, independently verified source/effect correctness, all prompt+output tokens and real GPU scheduling/queue/memory/latency**. Previous dedicated Thor multi-call and raw-data operations were **denied** and are not retried/reframed. Independently human-adjudicated role/purpose relevance, numeric-versus-text ordinal decisions in actual work outcomes and matched-compute modern-Qwen syntax training also remain open. The external software-agent experiment closes an important methodological gap—**final independently graded right-to-wrong switch costs when an online oracle is unavailable**—but does not finish the owner-wide research.
