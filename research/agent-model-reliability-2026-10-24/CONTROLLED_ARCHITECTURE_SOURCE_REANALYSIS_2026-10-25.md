## Actual original source and comparison

Kim et al., *Capable language models can outgrow the benefits of collaboration*, *Nature Machine Intelligence* (published July 24, 2026), DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01268-y, specifically the publicly released **Source Data Figs. 1 and 2 (download CSV)**. Source URL recorded from the publisher's original HTML: https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs42256-026-01268-y/MediaObjects/42256_2026_1268_MOESM2_ESM.csv . The paper reports 260 evaluated configurations, six agentic task families: BrowseComp-Plus, Finance Agent, PlanCraft, WorkBench, SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench, and up to five architectures: single agent (`SAS`), independent, centralized, decentralized and hybrid. Three model vendors and varying tiers were evaluated, with some absent benchmark cells due to model deprecation. The underlying paper matched **per-system reasoning-token compute ceilings, task prompts and available tools** across architectures, not every realized token count or call count; agent architectures incurred different observed coordination costs. The released source is per-*configuration mean* success, **not raw per-instance source/agent outputs**, so task-level error-correlation and significance testing are impossible from this CSV alone.

## Prespecified independent reanalysis

First, verify original URL and checksum, readable header/schema, numeric performance in [0,1], uniqueness of `(dataset, family, model, architecture)` and exact source count. If the publisher's data have unexpected columns, inspect and record rather than silently reinterpret. Normalize architecture naming only by explicit observed unique values. Do not add any synthetic cases, external web claims or changed gold labels.

Within **each matching dataset, family and model**, retrieve its actual single-agent baseline. Compare each reported MAS mode to that same baseline; exclude unmatched cells, count/report exclusions. Main outputs: (a) paired percentage-point delta by architecture and task dataset (mean, min/max, number of unique model-baseline pairs), (b) counts of gains, ties and losses for every architecture, (c) number of model/dataset matched cells where any MAS improves, where all MAS fail to improve and where a candidate best architecture changes by task family, (d) the mean best-MAS gain as a **post-hoc oracle upper bound** rather than a deployable policy, (e) a separate pairwise contrast for sequential PlanCraft versus divisible Finance Agent and realistic workplace WorkBench, and (f) whether higher single-agent baseline quality is associated with fewer gains, as an **exploratory** check of the authors' approximately 45% saturation criterion, without claiming independent test-set validation of that threshold.

Controls and fairness: preserve original same-dataset/model/compute baseline instead of comparing different model sizes or vendors. Do not treat multiple architectures under the same source model/dataset as independently sampled tasks. Per-model configuration means **do not establish confidence intervals of underlying task outcomes** and are reused within multiple matched architecture comparisons. Report any macro-averaging choices explicitly, and do not claim that the N=260 architectural configurations represent 260 independent user tasks. Cost analysis is limited to **published, source-cited, original measured full-call/token/turn numbers**; CSV without call-level token/latency cannot support a new model-specific cost-effectiveness claim. Do not equate a same-per-system-token-ceiling study with equal *realized latency/cost* across architectures. If the released data omit exact per-instance labels or GPU metrics, preserve that limitation.

Interpretation against a *second independent published source*, not pooled as a matching experiment: (i) Klang et al., *npj Health Systems* March 2026, compared **batch one-agent handling many heterogeneous jobs** versus isolated per-task workers; it reports large gains for orchestration at batch size eighty, but did **not** compare against single-agent **separate task-wise calls** under the same effective context per task; (ii) Sanabria, *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* July 2026, compared direct versus multiple specialist calls on one self-contained Mars rover scenario, which incurred much greater latency/tokens without a robust matched decision-accuracy benefit. These are different estimands; explicitly **do not combine their percentages** to claim one universal architectural effect.

A changed real-world SWAAG policy would only be justified by independent owner task/worker receipts and measured quality+total latency+GPU cost on actual resident models. This reanalysis can reveal which external task classes show benefits or harm, but cannot itself deploy a best architecture, choose a relevance representation or prove modern-Qwen grammar auxiliary training gain.
