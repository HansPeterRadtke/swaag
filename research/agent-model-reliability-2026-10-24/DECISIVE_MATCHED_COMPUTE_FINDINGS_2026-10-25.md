## New original source, provenance, declared control, real measured result

**Primary published evidence:** Kim, Y. et al., *Capable language models can outgrow the benefits of collaboration*, *Nature Machine Intelligence* volume 8, pp. 1157–1172, **24 July 2026**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01268-y . The study compares single-agent SAS with four multi-agent architectures across **six real agentic benchmark types** (BrowseComp-Plus, Finance Agent, PlanCraft, WorkBench, SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench), three vendor/model families and multiple model capability tiers. The paper explicitly says it matched **task prompts, tools and per-system reasoning-token ceilings** across architectures, varying the coordination design. Actual realized tokens, turns and latency nevertheless differ by design. The original publisher CC-licensed source CSV for Figs. 1 and 2 is versioned under `evidence/matched-compute-architecture/nature-machine-intelligence-2026-source-figs1-2-original.csv`, SHA-256 `402a9fea7430bf4dbf1645c8bafd7850811ffb5bb331568e8088d85c4dc9ffd9`. This provenance and pairing criteria were preregistered and pushed in `CONTROLLED_ARCHITECTURE_SOURCE_REANALYSIS_2026-10-25.md` *before downloading the CSV*. The CSV's original 260 rows have five fields: dataset, architecture, family, model, performance, and were retrieved directly from the publisher rather than inferred from plotted figures or assistant summaries.

**Independent evaluation:** the reproducible script `reanalyze_matched_compute_architecture_2026.py` verifies the entire original byte hash, original 260 rows, source schema, task/model/architecture uniqueness and score range, then matches each multi-agent result to the **same task benchmark, source model and vendor's actual single-agent result**. All **52** original model/dataset combinations have exactly one single-agent result and all four multi-agent alternatives, yielding **208** paired architecture contrasts without invented missing-data rows. Full source-matched contrasts (including negative results) are in `evidence/matched-compute-architecture/paired_architecture_outcomes_v1.json`. Per-cell aggregate performance is a mean over the paper's original benchmark instances; these 260 configurations are **not 260 independent user tasks or source proofs**. Neither per-case errors nor per-cell token/latency traces are included in this public CSV; it is impossible to reproduce the paper's uncertainty calculations or calculate a new per-cell cost-normalized SWAAG success rate from the CSV alone.

**Actual new paired results, expressed in absolute percentage points rather than confused with relative percentage gains:**

- **Finance Agent, nine models:** mean single-agent success **34.89%**. Centralized multi-agent **63.11%**, **+28.22 points** (equivalent to **+80.8% relative**), eight positive model-matched comparisons and one tie. Decentralized +26.00 points, hybrid +25.56 points, independent +19.78. Strong conditional benefit from coordination on the study's **decomposable financial reasoning** tasks.
- **PlanCraft, nine models:** single-agent **56.78%**. Independent multi-agent −39.75 points; centralized −28.61; decentralized −23.51; hybrid −22.17. For **all nine source models**, even the best-of-four MAS version never beat single-agent (eight losses, one tie). Clear harm from artificial coordination in this evaluated **sequential stateful planning** domain.
- **WorkBench, nine models:** single-agent **62.89%**; decentralized +3.56 points, centralized and hybrid −0.78, independent −7.22. Gains are small and mode-specific for the realistic-workplace benchmark.
- **BrowseComp-Plus, nine models:** single-agent **31.77%**; decentralized +2.91 points, hybrid +1.81, centralized +0.07, independent −11.16. No uniform multi-agent architecture winner even among knowledge/retrieval tasks.
- **SWE-bench Verified, eight models:** centralized −1.25, decentralized −3.13, hybrid −0.63 and independent −6.25 percentage points averaged across source models. Individual models vary, and the benchmark has only 20 source cases per source model/configuration; do not overinterpret mean differences.
- **Terminal-Bench, eight models:** independent +1.88 points, centralized −6.25, decentralized −1.88, hybrid −3.75. Different tool workflows require different coordination choices; broad all-agent claims would be ungrounded.

Across **all 52 matched model/dataset cells**, independent teams improved eighteen and lost thirty-two comparisons (two ties), with a mean delta **−7.31 points**. Centralized teams improved twenty-three and lost twenty-six (three ties), mean **−1.34 points**. Decentralized teams improved twenty-four and lost twenty-four (four ties), mean **+0.78 points**. Hybrid teams improved twenty-four and lost twenty-five (three ties), mean **+0.09 points**. Equally weighting these configurations is an explicit *macro-aggregation* choice, **not an estimate of SWAAG task-mix performance**. Picking whichever of four architectures hindsight says was best for every model/dataset shows gains in thirty cells, losses in eighteen and ties in four, with a hindsight mean +4.93 points, but **that is an unattainable oracle without a separately validated routing decision** and must not be described as a deployable selection system.

An exploratory audit of the paper's proposed capability-saturation concept found that among the 26 configurations whose single-agent mean success was **at most 45%**, *some* hindsight-chosen MAS beat SAS in **21** (81%); when baseline success was **above 45%**, at least one beat SAS in only **nine of 26** (35%). This supports a strong **directional task/model interaction**, not a validated cutoff for resident Qwen or SWAAG. The original article's reported **94%** predictive validation used a more specific fitted **baseline-by-team-size decision rule** on sixteen software/terminal configurations; directly plugging a threshold into the hindsight-best-of-four comparison is **not the same experiment**. A naive threshold sign audit on those sixteen means happens to agree fourteen times, not the publication's fifteen, because the definitions and decision rules differ. It would be scientifically incorrect to call this a reproduction failure or a new external prospective validation.

## Genuine total-cost evidence and why the apparent study conflict is not a contradiction

The Nature Machine Intelligence source says each **system** received the same compute-ceiling and tools. It reports realized additional reasoning turns **+58% independent**, **+263% decentralized**, **+285% centralized** and **+515% hybrid** versus SAS; in the fully cost-tracked subset, SAS averaged **7.2 reasoning turns**, decentralized **26.1**, centralized **27.7**, hybrid **44.3**. The paper describes overall success-per-token efficiency falling from **67.7 successes per 1,000 tokens SAS** to **21.5 centralized**, **23.9 decentralized** and **13.6 hybrid** under its definition. These are study-reported aggregates from the primary text, not reconstructed from the released performance-only CSV and **not measured SWAAG GPU latency**. The paper's positive finance gains can justify additional coordination for value-critical decisions, but they are not free.

A second **peer-reviewed**, different workload result directly explains why “one call is always cheaper” is also wrong. Klang et al., *Orchestrated multi agents sustain accuracy under clinical-scale workloads compared to a single agent*, *npj Health Systems* **9 March 2026**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s44401-026-00077-0, tested **batches** of 5–80 heterogeneous retrieval/extraction/dosing tasks. Its single-agent baseline placed the **whole batch** into one agent allowed up to **ten times the batch size** in model turns, while multi-agent orchestration routed each independent task to its own tool-specialist. At eighty tasks the reported pooled exact-match accuracy was **65.3% coordinated versus 16.6% monolithic**; at the largest GPT-4.1-mini batch, the study reports about **60,000 total coordinated tokens versus 3.9 million** with the monolithic baseline, a **65-fold** comparison. This proves that **whole-batch context overload and repeated gigantic history** can make isolating independent jobs dramatically better in that implementation. However, this study does **not** compare against a strong third baseline of **separate single-model task-wise calls with equally compact per-task prompts, and without extra agents**. The 65-fold improvement therefore **cannot be assigned entirely to multi-agent reasoning or proven necessary for SWAAG**, and its exact-match task primitives are not equivalent to a complex code project.

Conversely, Sanabria, *OpenAI single-agent LLM architecture reduces computational overhead relative to multi-agent orchestration in a simulated Mars rover decision-support benchmark*, *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* **6 July 2026**, https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2026.1877762, tested **one concise self-contained rover decision**, 100 source-controlled synthetic scenarios repeated five times. For GPT-4o, direct accuracy **81.0%** versus four-specialist/orchestrator **73.4%**, mean response time **2.32 vs 11.83 seconds**, and **458 versus 2,273 tokens**. For GPT-5.5, **97.4% vs 93.4%**, **6.06 vs 35.59 seconds**, **548 vs 3,160 tokens**. Overall decision-accuracy gaps were not consistently significant after the authors' scenario-clustered multiple-comparison correction; token/latency overhead was robust. This proves that a synthetic self-contained decision often **does not warrant multiple paid semantic calls**, not that independent subprojects should share one swollen context. Neither paper isolates the impact of a *single* extra relevance-filter call versus a *single* direct answer to the **same exact SWAAG goal**.

These source-driven distinctions align with the previous resident Thor experiments: the Qwen3.8 original action study found simplified `tool_calls`-only output within **one call** reduced measured calculator-call latency from **25.75 to 11.00 seconds**, and two-tool-call latency from **28.81 to 13.88 seconds**, while those selected decisions remained correct. The local full-three-recording versus per-recording extract-plus-synthesis Qwen study correctly settled four central claims under both approaches, but the serial hierarchy took **145.36 versus 116.81 seconds** and **14,961 versus 13,605 total input/output tokens**. In six source-exact SWAAG questions, model-selected evidence **stage one alone** consumed approximately the same tokens/time as direct completion; the second stage was not finished. That is a local negative lower-bound on extra call overhead, **not** a globally general comparison. No new Qwen experiment was run today; the earlier requested multi-call batch and raw-journal transfers were denied and have not been rerouted or replayed. Thor's disk was about 98% occupied and active production remains higher priority.

## Direct verdicts for original questions and exact unresolved scientific boundary

**Context for user versus worker:** a single rank does not carry context for all tasks. Preserve current original owner question, source/provenance, operative constraints, any authoritative later correction, actual tool calls/results and worker-state freshness. For the user-facing answer select material necessary to answer *what was asked*, not the details of work mechanics by default. Worker calls may need all details the user need not hear. A worker source result must be traceable to the question/tool that produced it. Direct evidence from SciFact showed entire expert rationales often missing from an apparently relevant sentence selection; do not assume that retrieving some relevant units completes a premise set. This is a strong independent **evidence availability** result, not proof of successful natural-language agent action.

**Numeric versus ordinal relevance:** both schemes gave three of five exact matches on **researcher-labeled** SWAAG original-report cases; both had extreme opposite-direction errors, and the difference in ordinal mean absolute error was statistically and scientifically too uncertain to use as policy. Primary rating-indeterminacy research (Guerdan et al., NeurIPS 2025, https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a309239c11a28c597d050bd4a1752d32-Abstract-Conference.html) shows independently reviewed response sets can be more appropriate than a single forced-choice label. Favor verifiable role-specific facets and an internally sortable representation without inferring calibrated factual truth or universal label thresholds. No decisive human-reviewed current-Qwen comparison has been performed.

**Combined versus specialist calls:** **two separate questions**: delegating multiple independent substantive jobs to isolated workers can protect task context and improve results at scale; splitting one short semantic answer into several serial model calls often increases latency with no guaranteed correctness gain. The matched-compute Nature Machine Intelligence reanalysis now supplies a **stronger empirical task-conditional source base**: finance specialization large positive, sequential planning strongly negative, software/workplace mixed or small. Current local Qwen first spoken response should remain **one capable model call** under Infra constraints; separately schedulable background workers may be divided by actual task/dependency boundaries rather than forcing one model to solve a global batch. Small model versus strong model and quantization effects are still confounded locally.

**Review/ensembles:** different model or retrieval methods can uncover alternatives but errors are correlated; reviewer votes do not become independent evidence. In the SciFact source retrieval train set, distinct BM25/TF-IDF methods **both missed complete gold rationale sets in 96 of 564** claim/paper pairs and simple fusion worsened the stronger single ranker; a third positional signal yielded modest gains whose paired interval included zero. This measures source retrieval, not factual Qwen reviewer error correlation. Use verifier feedback, real task receipts, executable checks, source evidence and explicit conflicting premises; call second models only on ambiguous/high-value failures where measured utility justifies cost.

**Syntactic/grammar auxiliaries:** conditional **YES** on some actual controlled tasks. Du et al., ACL 2020, https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.591/ found reduced heldout perplexity with jointly trained syntactic distances; other transformer grammar work found both sentence-level gains and document-level penalties, and supervised intermediate parsing has not uniformly benefited downstream semantic understanding. No modern resident Qwen model was retrained against matched architecture, data, FLOPs and verified natural-language heldouts, so a universal local win is absent. The tiny synthetic GRU pilot with 50% accuracy is not transferable contrary evidence.

**Operational conclusion:** preserve the direct capable user-facing call with a compact authoritative context; perform structured side-effect-safe tool and retrieval operations without wasting serial LLM calls where deterministic methods suffice; partition truly independent *background* work into separate task contexts while respecting shared GPU and model admission; introduce extra reasoning/review stages only when source-verified end-to-end correctness warrants their *entire* latency and tokens. Local evidence for the exact best auto-decomposition trigger, human-relevance format and correlated Qwen reviewer gains remains incomplete. A decisive next **owner-work** experiment would require a genuine original agent-task corpus with independent accepted worker receipts/grounded answers and multiple actually complete same-budget direct-versus-specialist trajectories. This study's public CSV cannot prove that without original task-level traces, and denial of previous transport/actions cannot be bypassed. **Research remains PARTIAL, while this independent source-data reanalysis is finished and reproducible.**

## New independently permitted reviewer-role and correlated-error analysis, round three, October 25

**Scientific target:** The owner asks whether a higher-capability critic/orchestrator should be trusted more than smaller workers, whether diverse additional agents actually bring independent evidence, and whether their gains justify additional computation. This is an independent **exploratory** analysis of the published paper's two *additional* original sources; it is **not** the previously denied `REAL_INSTANCE_CORRECTNESS_PROTOCOL` creation, does not recreate that document, and did not repeat the denied write or previously uncertain effects. Source provenance, code and outcomes are retained in the existing isolated SWAAG research branch, without any new model inference or production changes.

The primary source remains Kim et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* (July 2026), https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01268-y. The authors' immutable [Zenodo release v2.1.3](https://zenodo.org/records/20388843), original zip SHA-256 `3b6752af8271db7319b1b23dbbe93733bd13e585c87e6f1abc4a17e15b96dc35`, contains 1,800 per-instance SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench original task-completion records (original file SHA-256 `c7bde9eac1f28a29397c10ab10e29f0962595e9dd45f17aedc2d309289b12007`). The publisher's separately released **Source Data Extended Data Fig. 2**, https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs42256-026-01268-y/MediaObjects/42256_2026_1268_MOESM4_ESM.csv, SHA-256 `df497545c1f007ca8a6c1a9d41eb26602b1060a83f99b0ed0055919653f77c3f`, contains 21 **heterogeneous-team configuration means** across three original model families. The original Zenodo zip and 21-row CSV were independently retrieved, hash-validated and kept in Nitro's private scratch, not embedded or modified in Git. The prior published Fig. 1/2 source CSV is already in the research branch. Original source data are not invented.

`analyze_verified_error_dependence_2026.py` verifies all three source hashes and original schema, checks that `resolved` represents original task success and that every positive has an output, and recomputes **all 70 exactly matching per-configuration figure means** from the task records. The archive's `success` column is zero in every single row and cannot be used as a grader (doing so would falsely claim all architectures failed). A further 20 configurations have **nonidentical exact model identifiers** in the figure and per-instance sources (e.g. versioned Claude 4.5 and new Gemini 3); the primary outcome comparison retains only the **seven exact-model-ID matches** per task domain, with 20 identical original instance IDs per model and all five architectures: **140 model/task units per domain**, not 140 independent unique tasks. No manual alias guessing or source relabelling was performed. Every measured outcome is from the external suite's original completion grader, not an LLM opinion. This extension uses a different estimand from the previously blocked attempt to create a per-instance pairwise-control protocol: **cofailure dependence and heterogeneous role assignment**, not a new claim of significance for SAS-versus-MAS mean accuracy.

**New measured architecture cofailure:** Among the four multi-agent architectures on the same SWE-bench task and model, their pairwise binary success/failure phi correlations range **0.595–0.682**; on Terminal-Bench **0.554–0.651**. Pairing SAS with an alternative, correlations range **0.523–0.567** on SWE-bench and **0.332–0.612** on Terminal-Bench. These positive dependencies are **descriptive, not per-model LLM critic error correlation**: the same source task appears across models, all five architectures and vendors, with substantial shared difficulty. At least **four of the twenty SWE-bench tasks**, and **seven of the twenty Terminal-Bench tasks** were not solved by *any* of the **seven source-matched model versions under any of the five architectures**. Repeatedly assigning almost the same task to more coordinating agents plainly failed to solve those specific cases. This is a stronger factual boundary than 'different models agree, therefore their answer is true'. It still provides **no actual generated review messages**, so no majority-vote or external-reviewer calibration/precision can be measured. Without seeing the agent candidates, even a majority of task-success flags is a hindsight oracle, not a usable ensemble verdict.

**New measured model-placement evidence on the independent 21-row heterogeneity source:** The author tests **BrowseComp-Plus research QA**, with 100 task instances per released architecture/family/configuration; there are no task-level paired records or separate role-wise latency/tokens for this subset. In **centralized** systems, **low-capability lead/orchestrator plus high-capability subagent** beats **high-capability orchestrator plus low-capability subagent** by **2 percentage points OpenAI**, **5 points Anthropic**, and **5 points Gemini**. In **decentralized** systems, mixed-capability teams beat an all-high-capability team by **3 points OpenAI**, **10 points Anthropic**, but **lost 1 point Gemini**. The article explicitly interprets this as high-capability *subagents* being more useful than high-capability *orchestrators* in this limited experiment, not an axiom that a weaker coordinator is superior or that a larger judge should always control smaller executors. Compared with strong-only centralized systems, the high-orchestrator/low-worker mixed layout loses 15 points on OpenAI and 19 on Gemini, but differs by family for Anthropic. This **contradicts automatic 'give the biggest reviewer more weight'** as a universal design rule. It supports allocating the strongest available model to the role with the difficult semantic task, under actual cost and task constraints; **Qwen reviewer placement remains unmeasured**.

**Cost limitations:** The published paper gives full architecture-level overhead: SAS 7.2 reasoning turns in the cost-tracked subset versus 26.1 decentralized, 27.7 centralized, and 44.3 hybrid; reported success-per-1,000-token efficiency declines from 67.7 for SAS to 21.5 centralized, 23.9 decentralized and 13.6 hybrid under its metric. Those are original **paper-level aggregates**, not new per-task measurements; **per-instance original archive lacks token counts and wall-clock latency, and 21-row role data lack compute breakdown**. The reported `num_steps` per task is not a trustworthy proxy for complete token or GPU cost across architectures because a failed or outputless run may terminate early and step accounting differs by mechanism. Consequently it is **not possible to compute quality improvement per euro or minute for the observed strong-subagent/strong-orchestrator role variants**. Do not convert a modest source success difference into a cost-optimized recommendation.

**Actual useful conditional answer:** For SWAAG's fast user-facing turn, retain the already mandated *one capable initial model call* with fresh authoritative state and exact original user request; no extra paid 'rate relevance' call is justified by current local Qwen trials. Route independent research/programming subjobs to specialist workers when the work naturally separates and final effects can be independently tested; choose stronger reasoning for the most semantically demanding step, rather than according to whether it is named 'orchestrator' or 'critic'. On disagreements, request actual tool evidence, source quotes, failed test details and later owner corrections before deciding which model to trust. Do **not** treat correlated agents, high role count or same-label votes as proof. The earlier paired current Qwen results, 5 matched numeric/category ratings and model-evaluator mistakes are unchanged; their source anchors are not blinded human labels. Modern-Qwen syntactic auxiliary training remains separate untested work despite positive and negative peer-reviewed evidence in other architectures.

**Specific remaining experiment:** independent original SWAAG worker/foreground cases with complete original request, authoritative source and already verified test/side-effect receipts; on genuinely idle resident models, compare the *same case* with one direct call, correctly executed source-preserving specialist chains and selective externally grounded reviewer intervention, counting **all** calls, latencies, input/output tokens, model queue time and finished correctness. The current external source files cannot establish those missing locally actionable differences. No additional Calendar event, model service restart or previously denied data transfer was performed.
