# SciFact source-evidence retrieval baselines — preregistered before execution, 2026-10-25

Owner question: Does an agent's contextual evidence selector preserve the original information required to reason correctly, and what happens when sources are long, contradictory or semantically similar? This experiment measures **source evidence retrieval** against external expert rationale annotations, not final Qwen answer correctness and not subjective user-message importance. Previous non-replayed Qwen trials remain untouched. The previously denied private source transfer to Thor remains denied; this work runs strictly on the original Nitro source archive with a single CPU thread and lower process priority. No remote GPU, service or production worker is altered.

Original source and official evaluation: SciFact (Wadden et al., EMNLP 2020, https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/), canonical source archive `https://scifact.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/release/latest/data.tar.gz`, locally verified SHA-256 `11c621288d41ac144d29b13b0f8503b3820b7d6e8b1f6ff24dff335c196d76be`. Official source schema/evidence sets: https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/data.md ; official scoring: https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/evaluation.md . The **development split** contains three hundred original scientific claims, and hundreds of expert-labelled SUPPORT/CONTRADICT rationale sets against abstracts from the fixed corpus of 5,183 papers. Only claim text is the retrieval query. Only observed expert-labelled claim/document pairs are evaluable for within-abstract evidence recall; unlabeled documents must **not** be called negative or irrelevant. Any multi-sentence gold rationale is an atomic set: selecting only part does not count as recovered. Multiple rationale alternatives are OR, not the union of all evidence spans. Corpus and claim text remain private; source hashes, metrics, and source IDs rather than full paper passages may be checked into public project research documents.

Two **distinct stages** are evaluated, on all eligible dev examples rather than a chosen favorable small sample.

First, source-document retrieval from the entire 5,183-abstract corpus: compare unsupervised TF-IDF word uni/bigram cosine ranking with BM25 word-unigram ranking, both fitted on the *unlabeled original corpus* without using SciFact expert labels. Claim source text alone is the query; neither cited document IDs nor gold rationale data enter the scorer. For each claim having at least one gold annotated evidence abstract, report probability of retrieving any annotated gold abstract within top one, five, ten, twenty and fifty. Also report mean known-gold-document recall at those budgets. These are *known evidence* metrics: incomplete annotation means precision and unknown-document false-positive claims are not justified. The full corpus is a real stage, unlike the previous oracle-document experiments.

Second, within each expert-annotated claim/abstract pair, rank its **exact original sentences** and select top one, two, three, four or five under the same claim query. Compare an unsupervised TF-IDF word uni/bigram selector, a corpus-statistics BM25 unigram selector, a simple exact word-overlap heuristic, first-K positional selection, last-K positional selection, and deterministic random ranking. No scientific rationale label may be used to fit or rank any model. The expert rationale is used *only after ranking* to score whether **at least one entire documented rationale alternative** is included in selected IDs. This is not the same as evidence label prediction or a valid claim judgment. Report complete-set retrieval at every K, budget-dependent coverage of single-sentence and multi-sentence rationales, number of passages evaluated, and the fraction of original sentence text retained. The oracle upper-bound is the smallest full annotated rationale set; it is **not an agent-usable method**. Score SUPPORT versus CONTRADICT separately. Report rank parity failures and a deterministic sample of missed case IDs without publishing abstract text. Use a claim-clustered (not independent pair-wise) 1,000-resample bootstrap to describe the uncertainty of top-three retrieval *difference* between BM25/TF-IDF and first-sentence selection, with original data held fixed. Do not infer that a positive bootstrap interval proves a downstream LLM improvement.

Only source development labels and source-exact annotations are used; no training on dev labels, changing hyperparameters after seeing results, random-seed fishing, or heldout model selection. Algorithms and hyperparameters are frozen: `TfidfVectorizer` lowercased word uni/bigram with sublinear term frequency and L2 normalization, scikit-learn default word tokenizer; word-unigram BM25 with k1=1.2,b=0.75, positive Robertson IDF `log(1+(N-df+0.5)/(df+0.5))`; word-overlap count uses the same lowercased alphabetic tokenization; deterministic ties break by source position or document ID. The lexical IDF is fit on full unlabeled source corpus for document retrieval and on full unlabeled candidate evidence-abstract sentences for sentence retrieval. Single-thread CPU, `nice 15`, no model weights or downloads beyond the previously verified public source dataset. Source sets/predictions must remain reproducible by file/source SHA-256, IDs, ranking algorithm and fixed seed `20261025`.

Expected limits: SciFact rationale annotations can be incomplete, even when original abstracts are useful; documents are preselected at the sentence stage (oracle abstract retrieval), not end-to-end in the sentence stage; a lexical ranker can retrieve expert evidence but still be unable to determine SUPPORT versus CONTRADICT, authority or conditional scope; an LLM may reason correctly using different sentences. Simple word-overlap, a supervised cross-encoder, and the assembled full-text input are not matched-compute LLM competitors. A source-correct selector is a necessary interface component, not an entire solution to the owner's agent harness quality problem. No general numeric versus textual LLM relevance format, ensemble-error-correlation or auxiliary grammar-training conclusion follows from this CPU benchmark. Their earlier qualified findings stay in the original research reports.
