## Original recording questions and direct conditional answers

Context relevance must be evaluated relative to the consuming call: **what the user wants answered**, **which exact source/tool facts a worker needs to complete the requested action**, or **which worker status/dependencies the orchestrator needs to make an authorized decision**. Preserve original request, source identifiers, time/version/quotation scope, negations, superseding corrections and tool-call purpose. A fragment's similarity to the topic alone does not prove it is sufficient evidence. The earlier expert-annotated SciFact source study independently demonstrated that document retrieval and preserving a **complete original evidence rationale** are separate, imperfect requirements. The ICLR 2025 [LongMemEval](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d813d324dbf0598bbdc9c8e79740ed01-Abstract-Conference.html) benchmark directly tests extraction, multi-session inference, temporal reasoning, **knowledge updates and abstention** on 500 manually curated questions, and reported substantial degradation on sustained histories; its indexing/retrieval/reading optimizations can improve its tested memory Q&A. This supports separating authoritative current user turns from stale historical quotes while retaining exact raw provenance. It is not a measured SWAAG improvement.

**Numeric vs named ordinal relevance:** no format winner on locally loaded Qwen. The five previously matched authentic SWAAG same-case/role/seed/model pairs scored **three of five exact** against one researcher's disputed zero/eight necessity labels for each representation; numeric mean ordinal error **2.0** versus named categories **3.2**, with tiny-sample resampling compatible with either direction. These are **not blinded human annotations** and score agreement is not worker/action accuracy. NeurIPS 2025 [Guerdan et al.](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a309239c11a28c597d050bd4a1752d32-Abstract-Conference.html) demonstrate that rating indeterminacy can make forced-choice evaluation select suboptimal judges on eleven real rating tasks. An ordered machine-readable score is usable for prioritization, but score levels must have explicit task/rubric meaning; acceptable sets of ratings and *downstream source-based acceptance* matter more than a forced single number or word.

**Combined vs specialist calls:** a narrower **output contract within one call** wins locally on several studied action tasks. A generic first-stage semantic relevance classifier is not a reliable improvement; end-to-end multi-stage correctness and total model/queue cost must be checked before changing call graphs. This continuation discovered an already completed Qwen3.8 Q8 study that directly transfers a large selection of old SmolVLM prompts without changing the original source prompts/schemas. Details and audit follow. A new July 2026 peer-reviewed controlled synthetic rover decision-support study, [Sanabria, Frontiers in Robotics and AI](https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2026.1877762), ran **100 labelled simulated scenarios with five repeated evaluations per architecture** using GPT-4o and GPT-5.5. With GPT-4o, single vs multiple specialists achieved decision-accuracy **81.0% vs 73.4%**, latency **2.32 s vs 11.83 s**, and mean tokens **458 vs 2,273**. With GPT-5.5, **97.4% vs 93.4%**, latency **6.06 s vs 35.59 s**, and tokens **548 vs 3,160**. **The corrected scenario-clustered, Holm-adjusted decision-accuracy comparisons did not establish statistically significant winner**, while overhead differences were significant. These are synthetic rover-policy decisions with near-zero exact hazard-list F1 and do not establish an identical accuracy effect in SWAAG programming, tools, memory or Qwen. Nevertheless the study is strong independent corroboration that adding specialist calls can cost roughly five-to-six times as much with no demonstrated aggregate accuracy gain on compact, self-contained inputs.

**Review/ensembles:** externally verified counterexamples and source-dependent checks are valuable; votes alone are not. The held-out original SciFact lexical-ranker experiment found **ninety-six shared complete-evidence misses among 564 expert-labelled claim/paper cases**, with phi around **0.786**. Naive TF-IDF/BM25 fusion was worse than BM25 alone at equal context budget; an independently biased sentence-position signal gave a modest gain. This measures **lexical evidence availability, not LLM reviewer errors**. On real Qwen SWAAG work, Coder Q4 incorrectly elevated an old user quote over a later authoritative correction; Qwen3.8 Q8 got the same question right, with ~1.55 versus ~4.95 seconds full-answer latency. This is **one observed independently source-checkable cross-model disagreement**, not a reliable per-task conditional routing policy or reviewer-correlation estimate. [Kamoi et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/) report why unsupported prompted self-correction is unreliable; externally grounded feedback is the relevant testable intervention.

**Grammar training:** **conditional YES** for supervised syntax/structural bias improving particular language-model heldout outcomes, **NO** proof of universal improvement or modern installed Qwen improvement. [Du et al., ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.591/) report lower language-model perplexity from syntactic-distance supervision on tested Penn/Chinese Treebanks; [Sartran et al., TACL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.81/) report sentence-level syntax-sensitive gains alongside a document-level modeling disadvantage; [Glavaš and Vulić, EACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/) find limited/inconsistent broad semantic-understanding benefits from supervised parsing. The prior tiny local synthetic GRU experiment is not transferable evidence; no equal-FLOP modern Qwen auxiliary-training comparison exists. Grammar tags in a prompt are not that experiment.

## Verified pre-existing Qwen technique-transfer study, NOT an additional inference run

Source on Thor (read-only): `/data/var/swaag-benchmarks/model-decomposition-27b-20261025/technique-transfer-findings.md`, `technique-score-v2.json` (SHA-256 `5e58fc3b8d0ae1febdca5fd7140898ad4c8466f2c243ae000a49bb57a65765b3`), the same directory's per-case original prompts, schemata, stored Qwen completions and external-scoring script. This new agent-owned study had already executed **61 resident Qwen3.8-27B Q8 completions across 46 selected historical case variants**. Its source-specific scorer marked **54 semantic passes, 7 semantic failures; all 61 outputs were schema valid**. These are deliberately failure-selected operational fixtures with repeated greedy seeds, **not 61 independent natural SWAAG jobs**, and its scorer contains strict manually specified field/subset criteria. Never infer from its 54/61 proportion a general model accuracy rate or extrapolate to new owner requests. Small SmolVLM2-2.2B Q4 outcomes were recorded under another runtime/model family and a mix of greedy/sampled seeds; architectural or quantization causality cannot be isolated.

At identical Qwen model/backend and **identical prompt-input token counts** across variants, the calculator action's full schema produced the correct calculator call in both recorded greedy runs, as did the restricted `tool_calls`-only schema. But **mean wall latency dropped from 25.75 s to 11.00 s**, with input tokens identical at **1,649**, while generated tokens dropped **166 to 52**. Thus **narrowing one call's required output** reduced observed response time by **57.3%** without sacrificing correctness on this narrow task. The two-tool action similarly passed in both full and restricted variants and fell from **28.81 s to 13.88 s** (mean of two greedy runs), with the same **1,736 input tokens** and generated output reduced **188 to 73 tokens**. This is approximately a **51.8%** latency reduction. Neither result required serializing another LLM call. Conditions are very narrow and the repetitions were largely deterministic under greedy decoding; don't attach unwarranted confidence limits.

Other results are mixed rather than a victory for split tasks. The strict full response-relevance rewrite failed on both Qwen greedy runs **even though the main answer was correct**, because it hallucinated/rephrased omitted details (including an incorrect process identifier); its source-unit selector was correct **twice**, but this **only establishes selection of two exact source IDs**, not a validated two-call final answer. The simplified `summary_only` variant lost the still-active **never-delete-original-archive** instruction in both repeated runs, while a more structured fact inventory retained the prescribed facts. A one-next-capability tool discovery primitive wrongly chose `echo` when `search_repo` was required. Other source-unit and status variants that the small model failed were handled correctly by Qwen. The Qwen summary's seven strict failures include these different failure types; complete semantic fidelity cannot be inferred from schema membership. The useful generalization is **operation- and model-dependent semantic task shape**, not smaller schemas or multiple LLM calls always winning.

## A real three-original-recording full versus hierarchical Qwen cost comparison

Source on Thor: `/data/var/swaag-benchmarks/model-decomposition-27b-20261025/long_recordings`. The recorded task evaluates policy context from **three original owner Infra recordings dated October 12, 14 and 15**, totalling about sixty-four thousand characters. This is **not** the October seventeenth/eighteenth original question and should not be marketed as a new independent validation of it. With resident Qwen3.8 Q8, a single full-context call (`focused-v2-full.json`) consumed **13,006 evaluated input tokens plus 599 generated tokens** and **116.81 seconds**. Separate original-recording fact extracts (`focused-v2-oct12.json`, `focused-v2-oct14.json`, `focused-v2-oct15.json`) plus the final synthesis (`focused-v2-hierarchical.json`) consumed a **combined 14,129 evaluated input plus 832 generated tokens** and a **total 145.36 seconds**. At the same source question, both approaches returned the four central negative Boolean policy requirements correctly, but explanatory qualification and citation fidelity had different defects. **The hierarchical chain took 24.4% longer and about 10.0% more total tokens** on that task; counting only its 46.35-second final synthesis would be a misleading cost comparison. This experiment has **one central question** and model generation is affected by output length, schema and shared GPU load; it is not a population significance result.

The same source folder's broader earlier extraction attempt must **not be recounted as a completed success**: the `architecture-split-oct14.json` extract hit output limit and had no parsed result after **161.4 s**, whereas an `architecture-full.json` answer was completed after **134.03 s** but contained garbled free-text list qualifications. Both are negative fidelity/operational evidence; a constrained JSON result is insufficient proof that facts were preserved. The separately produced summaries/alternate prompts are *not* a balanced heldout dataset. Model VRAM consumption and GPU queueing are not recorded consistently, so total per-stage wall seconds and tokens are the verified cost dimensions here.

## A narrow but reproducible counterexample for context ordering

A separate pre-existing Qwen order-probe set is preserved under the same Thor research root's `adversarial-order/`. In a deliberately **tiny synthetic four-record** source-ID selection task with identical words and JSON schema, only the position of the relevant record moved. Coder-30B-A3B Q4 returned **all four record IDs** when the relevant record came first, but correctly returned only its one ID when it appeared last. This held for the one recorded greedy run and **three temperature-zero-point-two seeds** in each position (four wrong-first and four correct-last). Qwen3.8 Q8 selected the correct ID in **all eight** matched first/last and greedy/sampled runs. Shorter note- and response-source-unit order variants were correct at middle/last for both models. These are **controlled prompt-order counterexamples**, not a sample showing that last position is universally best. They establish that input order can interact with particular models and tasks even at about one hundred tokens, while the more capable model was robust on this **one** adversarial set. Do not use this to override source chronology or place old user messages ahead of current authoritative requests.
