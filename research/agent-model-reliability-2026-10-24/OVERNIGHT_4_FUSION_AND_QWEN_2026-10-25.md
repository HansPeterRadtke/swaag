## Original question: does combining multiple selection methods preserve more correct evidence?

**At a fixed budget of the first three selected sentences, on 564 expert-annotated claim/paper pairs:** first-three baseline **25.71%**, last-three baseline **66.31%**, seeded random **46.99%**, TF-IDF **78.01%** (440/564) and BM25 **80.85%** (456/564). Equal-weight RRF of **BM25 and TF-IDF alone** reached **79.79%** (450/564), so it actually **lost six complete expert rationales relative to BM25**. Fixed alternating BM25/TF-IDF scored **80.14%**. Adding a third, differently biased **reverse-position ranking** to equal-weight RRF gave **83.33%** (470/564). This means **fourteen more** recovered complete rationales than BM25 at the same three-sentence budget, not fourteen more verified final scientific answers. The three-way score retained around **39% of source sentence characters**, similar to BM25's **39%**, so the advantage is not explained by more source text. The fixed TF-IDF plus positional ranking achieved **81.21%**; BM25 plus positional ranked **82.27%**.

These results replicate the earlier development finding that naive agreement among similar lexical selectors does not automatically help. The **independent train split** had TF-IDF/BM25 jointly successful on **428** pairs, jointly missing on **96**, with **twelve TF-IDF-only** and **twenty-eight BM25-only** successes. The observed paired binary hit/miss phi coefficient was **0.786**, lower than the earlier development set's 0.862 but still strong positive cofailure. **These are ranker retrieval errors, not errors made by different LLMs**, so do not claim measured Qwen ensemble dependence from this coefficient. Combining two strongly correlated lexical rankings can underperform the best single ranker at fixed context budget.

The original preregistered claim-clustered thousand-resample bootstrap reports a **five-point-three-two percentage-point** gain for three-way fusion versus TF-IDF, descriptive 95-percent interval **2.86 to 7.87 points**. The contrast with BM25 (two-point-four-eight points) was computed as a **post hoc complementary comparison**, with a descriptive interval **zero to 4.93 points**; it **does not exclude no advantage over BM25**, and should not be advertised as a statistically established improvement. The rankers differ in score distributions, the candidate method matrix is finite, and sentence-position bias is substantial in SciFact: last-three alone exceeds first-three. The train split's known-paper position signal transferred directionally from dev, but its scientific-abstract structure cannot justify using recency by default for SWAAG conversations, older constraints or real documents.

**On the specifically difficult multi-premise cases**, only **32 of 564** pairs had no one-sentence complete expert rationale. At a three-sentence budget, BM25 recovered a whole rationale in **9/32**, TF-IDF in **10/32**, and three-way fusion also in **10/32**. Most multi-sentence cases were still missing at least one necessary premise. This is independent negative evidence against assuming a high average ranker score means safe compaction. One can have correct retrieval of an individual highly related sentence while omitting the conjunction or exception that makes the original conclusion sound.

## Original question: combined source delivery versus sequential filtering

Source document retrieval was also evaluated from the **full corpus of 5,183 papers**, without supplying oracle source document IDs to the ranker. Among the 505 annotated claims, TF-IDF placed a known source paper within its top ten for **89.90%**, BM25 for **93.47%**, and simple two-ranker RRF for **92.87%**. RRF did **not** improve on BM25, despite its favorable results in other information retrieval studies. In a source retrieval cascade using BM25's top ten documents and three sentences per candidate, BM25-only sentence selection kept some complete expert rationale for **389/505 claims**, or **77.03%**; using three-way lexical/position sentence selection raised this to **404/505**, or **80.00%**. Known evidence papers had already been retrieved for approximately **472/505** claims at the document stage, so **68** claims with a correct document available nevertheless lacked a complete annotated rationale after sentence filtering. This is a measurable loss between context-selection stages, **not** a demonstration that a multi-LLM chain improved final answer correctness.

At the source-evidence level, two stages—retrieve the right document, then select the necessary original sentences—are an appropriate useful *analysis*, and their errors compound. But these rankers use ordinary CPU text retrieval, **not separate semantic LLM calls**. An agent's decision to call another LLM must justify its **additional full prompt, generation, GPU queue latency and potential downstream premise loss** against a direct call that already contains adequate original facts. Earlier local Qwen source-grounding trials saved around 45–56% input tokens by using researcher-identified source passages, but there were **no completed model-select-then-answer chains** under production concurrency. Both the earlier 198-plus small-model decomposition study and current Qwen results contain negative evidence: narrow action-output schemas sometimes substantially help, abstract first-stage classifiers and naive source-ID selection can fail, and multi-call extraction can truncate or miss important conditions. This train-split CPU study does not settle which complex semantic call should be split or how many calls an actual user-facing task can tolerate.

## Numeric-versus-textual relevance and grammar-auxiliary outcomes

Earlier role-conditioned local Qwen relevance trials on authentic SWAAG engineering-report paragraphs showed **four exact matched numeric/category pairs**, with large opposite-direction errors relative to one researcher's *task-necessity anchors*. These anchors were not blinded independently human-adjudicated, so the observed model sensitivity to output form is real but no format is yet objectively more accurate for the owner's worker/user/orchestration goals. The external ARES example file had mixed or unlabeled duplicates and weak label-provenance constraints; it cannot replace a real human role-relevance corpus. There is no new valid numeric/category superiority conclusion from this retrieval ensemble study. A relevance number is an ordered priority, never a factual-truth probability.

The original grammar-auxiliary question is **conditionally answered by primary science** but remains untested on resident Qwen training: Du et al., *Exploiting Syntactic Structure for Better Language Modeling* (ACL 2020, https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.591/) trained next-word prediction jointly with syntactic-distance supervision and reported improved heldout perplexity on Penn/Chinese Treebank under its tested setting. Glavaš and Vulić (EACL 2021, https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/) report limited/inconsistent general semantic-language-understanding benefit from parsing intermediate training, so syntax supervision is **not** a universal shortcut. The old six-thousand-parameter synthetic GRU pilot with fifty-percent number agreement is too small to settle real transformer outcomes. No full-Qwen weight retraining or install was attempted; Thor production and disk limits continue to apply.

### Published independent feedback rather than majority voting

Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher (SIGIR 2009, https://doi.org/10.1145/1571941.1572114) report effective reciprocal rank fusion in their retrieval tasks; our frozen heldout data show that this claim is **not universal across different ranker combinations, datasets, and expert evidence metrics**. Kamoi et al., *When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?* (TACL 2024, https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/) emphasize reliable **external feedback** and identify common failures of unsupported prompted self-critique; Findeis et al., *Can External Validation Tools Improve Annotation Quality for LLM-as-a-Judge?* (ACL 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.779/) find gains for source- and tool-grounded evaluation in several domains but not every case. Tan et al. (EMNLP 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.238/) show self-consistent wrong responses can evade common detection. These original peer-reviewed results support using independent, checkable proof sources rather than self-reinforcing agreement, yet **do not substitute for a measured correlated-error matrix on the installed models**.

## Remaining research and engineering boundaries

The operational recommendation follows the evidence, not a permanent system redesign: keep the complete exact source outside the prompt, preserve authoritative user task/purpose, original tool request/result, source/revision/time scope, constraints and contradictions; select/retrieve complete premise **sets**, not just lexically similar isolated sentences; measure final user response and worker effect accuracy before deploying automated filtering; use model disagreement to direct a source check rather than add votes as proof. For fast speech, give the user-facing model its one capable initial call with a recent trustworthy state and active goals, while deep worker research and source verification can proceed separately without delaying that answer.

Unanswered by verified local experiments: independent, large authentic *agent-work* human labels for user-versus-worker-versus-orchestration relevance; balanced numeric versus ordered textual Qwen judgments on such labels; sufficient **completed** model-selected-vs-fused chains using fair multi-call total cost; actual expert-annotated SciFact/Qwen factual judgments after a specifically denied transfer (do not retry without a separately permitted path); enough independently resolved matched reviewer errors across models, seeds and equivalent prompts; controlled model-size/quantization isolation; and compute-matched current-transformer syntax training across natural-language heldouts. The preceding completed source-ranker benchmark does **not** close those questions.
