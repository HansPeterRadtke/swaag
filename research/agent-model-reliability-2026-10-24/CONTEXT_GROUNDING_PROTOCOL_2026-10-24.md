# Authentic SWAAG context-selection and final-answer comparison (frozen before inference)

Date: October 24, 2026, third continuation. Primary owner question: how does an agent harness assemble **the specific information required by each model call** so semantic task correctness improves, and does separating selection from answer generation *actually help* on local Qwen compared with a single combined context/answer model call? Historical developer labels alone are insufficient; this study measures final source-grounded answer correctness and total token/wall-clock costs.

## Frozen independent source facts and evidence scope

`context_grounding_v1.json` contains eight task questions, each with three concrete answer options, one author-defined expected answer anchored in exact pre-existing SWAAG engineering documentation, two exact identified source-support paragraphs and three source-exact distractors. Each passage retains source file, SHA-256 of full file and exact character offsets into it; `build_context_grounding_v1.py` regenerates these without source edits. The reports predate the new test and document genuine SWAAG observations on worker status, progress, old history, source attribution, tokenizer admission and compact foreground context. They are **authentic developer-investigation reports**, not direct original user utterances, not a natural distribution of production turns, and not a blinded human-rater gold set. Study questions/options were authored for this controlled evaluation; their answers are checkable against the original passages but do not establish independent external world truth beyond those archived reports. The two designated support paragraphs are a source coverage objective, not a formal guarantee every question semantically requires both paragraphs.

## Three conditions, same two loaded endpoints, outcome metrics

For each fixed task on the installed Thor Qwen3-Coder Q4 and Qwen3.8 Q8, compare:

* `full`: one model call sees the unchanged task question, its same three options and all five real source passages in frozen order.
* `model_select`: a constrained model first chooses up to three real passage IDs using the complete same task and same five passages; a separate model call answers using **only selected original verbatim passages** plus the unchanged question/options, not generated summaries. Score final answer correctness, selected-reference coverage and the cost of *both* model calls. This is the evidence-preserving specialist-call candidate.
* `gold_select`: one model call sees only the two researcher-identified source support passages, the original question and options. This is a **source-selection diagnostic upper bound**, not an independently human-certified optimal context or a deployable oracle.

No model weights, server configuration, or production source are changed. Each trial's order among the three conditions is deterministically shuffled before inference by task ID; two backend models are used sequentially, not as concurrent reviewers. Fixed greedy temperature zero, seed 17, thinking disabled, one native slot per server, strict JSON schemas with short outputs, identical original answer options and facts. Conditions intentionally vary input/context length; cost must include **all** model input, output tokens and inference call latencies, not just the final answer. The source selector's structural validity and recall of researcher-designated passages are distinct from semantic correctness; a selector that omits a designated passage can still yield the right answer, and the reverse is possible.

A success is an exact returned option equal to the historical source-backed expected answer. Record per task/model/condition exact output, schema validity, number of request stages, selected source IDs, support coverage, total prompt/completion/overall tokens, per-call and cumulative wall latency, resource admission state, and final correctness. Pair only completed cases under the same task/model; do not assign stopped capacity-gate trials a wrong-answer value. Errors from different model families cannot isolate quantization or trained capability. No statistical significance or universal winner is claimed from just eight study questions. A larger independently human-adjudicated, authentic-turn holdout with execution effects is still required to settle the owner's broad hypothesis.

## Hard workload safety and non-replay

Every inference uses existing native endpoint only after current SWAAG supervisor confirms no active sessions, no queued/running workers, **both** model servers idle and up-to-date, and native `/slots` idle; Thor available host memory and free disk must meet the existing conservative thresholds. There is no model restart, new model installation, service preemption, synthetic worker, user-task mutation or bulk data ingestion. Stop at first resource contention/uncertain tool result, persist exact pre-request outcome-unknown event and do not retry that trial in later iterations. `run_context_grounding_v1.py` keeps an append-only journal and never replays any ID already present. A new user/worker request may race the check by milliseconds; the check is a **snapshot admission guard**, not an exclusive lease. Run no long/large context case while the owner is using voice or Thor GPU is busy. Capping each trial's passages at roughly four thousand characters makes this a bounded first comparison, not an exhaustive 262-thousand-token context experiment.

## Comparison to independently published research

Human-annotated [GaRAGe (Findings ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.875/) reports challenges with selecting relevant grounding and faithful use; it does not benchmark this SWAAG setup. [Do RAG Systems Cover What Matters? (NAACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.301/) distinguishes core/background/follow-up facets and records missing core information. [Question Decomposition for RAG (ACL SRW 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-srw.32/) finds gains in evidence coverage and answer correctness from decomposed retrieval plus reranking on two multi-hop benchmarks. Conversely, [Lost-in-Retrieval (ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1089/) shows subquestion decomposition can omit key entities, harming the entire chain. [Long Context RAG Performance (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03538) reports that long context occupancy often harms accuracy rather than guarantee robust retrieval. This motivates a task-output measurement with exact source spans and total cost, not an automatic assumption that either more context or more calls is superior.

Use `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 -m unittest discover -s research/agent-model-reliability-2026-10-24 -p 'test_*.py' -q` for offline data/source validation, and on Thor run the frozen script in the private scratch path with `--max-new <= 12` and a **new** append-only journal. Results are copied back to the existing SWAAG research branch in their original byte form. This experiment does not re-run, repair or relabel previous failed Qwen extraction/selection trials.

Later research must test withheld-source controls, real original user/workflow context, actual worker output and user feedback, while protecting an untouched external holdout. Relying on output-token counts alone or the four-of-four diagnostic accuracy would overstate quality.

### Primary scientific synthesis relevant to the original owner recording

The owner was correct to separate **information essential to the agent's source-based work** from **information the user explicitly requests to hear**. Source passages, history and tool observations have role-, task-, source-, authority- and time-specific relevance. Current incoming user wording plus durable current goals and restrictions decide priority. For a simple user-facing reply, an authoritative recent state snapshot, required user constraints and the exact user message must arrive in the **one mandated initial semantic model call**. Do not insert serial relevance-classifier calls ahead of speech. For deep research/planning or an invoked tool, assemble the selected original source spans, the last exact tool request with why it was called, the returned payload's version/evidence/provenance, assumptions, negations/conditions, changed state and sufficient output budget; retain all other original material outside the model context with exact references. The deterministic harness may then filter/sort well-defined structured decisions, but **cannot by itself replace source-grounded semantic judging**. Important scope conflicts warrant fetching the original again. This is a source-grounded operating conclusion, not a claim the existing SWAAG runtime already implements every proposed quality mechanism flawlessly.

Specific primary results reinforce and qualify that approach. The human-annotated [GaRAGe benchmark (ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.875/) reports failures to restrict generated answers to relevant grounding passages. [Sub-question coverage (NAACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.301/) distinguishes core questions from background/follow-up and reports missing core components. [Question decomposition for RAG (ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-srw.32/) reports multi-hop retrieval and answer gains from decomposition **plus reranking**. [Lost-in-retrieval (ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1089/) documents entity omission in decomposition and motivates safeguards. [CheckEval (EMNLP 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.796/) improves rating reliability using decomposed binary attribute questions compared with subjective scalar approaches, but this does **not** directly compare installed local Qwen ordinal enums against integer fields. [Rating Roulette (EMNLP 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1361/) observes unstable LLM-as-judge repeated scoring. Accordingly, categorical-vs-numeric local reliability remains unknown beyond the earlier small paired observations, and neither method deserves universal deployment policy.

For cross-model criticism, [Diversity and Deliberation in Homogeneous LLM Prediction Polls (REALM 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.realm-1.85/) reports **0.910 residual forecast-error correlation** over 189 resolved snapshots and no detectable ensemble aggregate-accuracy improvement from standard deliberation in its evaluated predictive-forecast setting. That is striking empirical evidence against treating repeated correlated opinions as independent corroboration; it is **not** a measured error coefficient for Qwen, source extraction or our factual tasks. [Demystifying Multi-Agent Debate (Findings ACL 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1694/) reports task-conditioned benefits from diversity and confidence-aware debate, not naive agreement. The more general acceptable quality protocol is explicit source-span criticism, competing hypotheses, and independent external outcome checks. Missing evidence or agreement without verified facts must remain unresolved.

For auxiliary grammar training, existing primary studies and the previous preserved local toy remain unchanged. [Glavaš & Vulić (EACL 2021)](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/) find limited and inconsistent improvements from intermediate syntactic parsing for semantic language understanding; [Strubell et al. (EMNLP 2018)](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1548/) find task-specific SRL gains from linguistically informed attention; [Hu et al. (ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.478/) report positive results with hierarchical formal-language *pre-pretraining*, not the same as dependency-label auxiliary fine-tuning. The earlier six-thousand-parameter three-arm GRU synthetic experiment found no agreement-number accuracy gain; it remains **nontransferable** to modern Qwen weights. No external independent current-architecture grammar-auxiliary answer has been established locally.

### Primary literature relevant to choosing call shape and reviewer policy

`Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation` (Ammann et al., ACL SRW 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-srw.32/) reports improved retrieval and answer accuracy on MultiHop-RAG/HotpotQA with query decomposition **plus merged reranking**, relative to the paper's baselines—not a universal gain from additional calls. `Mitigating Lost-in-Retrieval Problems` (Zhu et al., ACL 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1089/) documents a contrary failure mechanism: missing entities in subquestion decomposition disrupt the subsequent answer. `Evaluating the Consistency of LLM Evaluators` (Lee et al., COLING 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.710/) explicitly studies self- and inter-scale inconsistency rather than assuming scalar/ordinal ratings transfer unchanged between prompts and models. `CheckEval` (Lee et al., EMNLP 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.796/) found +0.45 average inter-evaluator agreement for decomposed binary checklists in its evaluated text-generation tasks. This **does not mean one textual category is more accurate than one integer** or that binary checklist questions must require separate serial model calls.

`Too Consistent to Detect` (Tan et al., EMNLP 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.238/) directly evaluates repeated stochastic **same wrong answers** and finds that four mainstream detection methods struggle with these self-consistent mistakes. `From Belief Entrenchment to Robust Reasoning` (Oh et al., TACL 2026, https://aclanthology.org/2026.tacl-1.58/) finds homogeneous debate can reinforce incorrect shared prior beliefs and reports gains from deliberately more diverse perspectives **on its benchmark**. `Diversity and Deliberation in Homogeneous LLM Prediction Polls` (Gebele et al., REALM 2026, https://aclanthology.org/2026.realm-1.85/) reports a high mean residual error correlation (0.910) across 189 resolved forecasting snapshots; those data concern prediction polls and **are not evidence for a specific Qwen extraction-error coefficient**. The owner-recorded ensemble hypothesis therefore needs independent provenance/source verification, not majoritarian model voting. None of these published outcomes supplies the missing model-selected Qwen end-to-end trials.

## Original grammar-auxiliary question: stronger boundary from primary literature

`MicroBERT` (Gessler & Zeldes, MRL 2022, https://aclanthology.org/2022.mrl-1.9/) trained low-resource monolingual encoder models with auxiliary part-of-speech/dependency tasks and reported downstream improvements on seven low-resource languages, including gains up to 11 NER F1 points against its multilingual baseline. That comparison varies size/language/architecture and **cannot isolate a compute-matched benefit on a Qwen decoder**. Conversely, Glavaš & Vulić (EACL 2021, https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.270/) report limited/inconsistent downstream language-understanding benefit from syntactic intermediate training; Hu et al. (ACL 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.478/) show improved model loss and linguistic generalization after formal hierarchical-language **pre-pretraining**, including an approximately one-third training-token reduction at matched loss in a billion-parameter experiment. The interventions and baselines differ. A deployed Qwen Q4-versus-Q8 comparison is not a training-objective experiment; the local tiny GRU result cannot be generalized. The research justifies testing this only where training data, matching compute and independent heldouts are actually available, not installing more weights onto Thor's near-full disk.
