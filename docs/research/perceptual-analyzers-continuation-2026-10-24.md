## Actual new local measurements

The new CPU-only screenshot probe `tests/research/perceptual_image_only_probe.py` renders fresh GUI images from independently specified cases, then evaluates algorithms without reading the case label to predict the defect. Its screenshot *change* baseline compares raw pixels to a fixed reference with a mean absolute difference threshold; on eight rendered images it got **three of eight binary defect decisions correct**. All three real defect types (clipping, overlap, low contrast) were missed and two valid cases (dark theme and right-aligned text) were false positives: binary defect precision **zero of two**, recall **zero of three**. A difference signal is not a defect analyzer. In particular, a clipped string changed too few pixels to exceed the chosen threshold.

The Tesseract **5.3.4** + intended-text-contract comparator read screenshot pixels using OCR with `--psm 6`. On the same eight PNGs, it classified **seven of eight** correctly: three true defect detections, one false positive for a legitimate right-aligned label, no missed constructed defects, four true negatives. Defect precision **3/4**, recall **3/3**. Total observed OCR wall time approximately **19 seconds for eight images**, average about **2.4 seconds on this loaded Nitro**, using one CPU thread. The intended text is supplied separately by the test contract: this is **not a label-free image-only solution**, and missing OCR text cannot disambiguate clipping from low contrast, occlusion, or segmentation error. It remains unsuitable as an unreviewed sole acceptance gate. The independent gold manifest comes from fixture construction and has no blinded human annotations.

The same image-only ROI pixel contrast probe detected the low-contrast fixture but also flagged the legal right-aligned control because its chosen crop did not include enough glyph pixels. It is a diagnostic local-region measurement, not a portable contrast classifier. Real web/browser rendered screenshot fixtures were attempted using isolated Chromium profiles. Chromium terminated with `Failed to create a ProcessSingleton` before producing any screenshot. No GUI/browser end-to-end success is claimed and the failed step was not obscured by substituting PIL images.

The diagram pixel-line analyzer received four *raster* fixtures: two clean/legitimately recolored, one intentionally broken wire and one overprinted wire. An initial incorrectly thickness-normalized scan detected only **one of four**; this exact negative first-pass result is retained in `results-first-pass.json`. After debugging the scan implementation on the development fixtures, it classified **three of four**, correctly identifying the broken wire, but missed overprinted text. The corrected four cases are **development data, not independent heldout evidence**; a different font/scale/diagram family and semantic topology oracle must be tested before promotion.

The simplistic scene-space Z-boundary mechanical checker classified **three of four** procedural assemblies: it detected deliberate floor intrusion but mistakenly rejected a legitimately recessed mechanical track. This is geometry metadata, not an analysis of rendered pixels or machinery appearance. On a real existing forklift GLB (read only, approximately ten megabytes), standard-library validation examined **281** position-accessor bounding boxes for **281** meshes and found **zero** inverted or nonfinite bounds; four accessors were flat along an axis, which is not automatically a defect. The asset contains **479 nodes**. Geometrically well-formed bounds do not prove correct collision, scale, contact, industrial component arrangement, texture, optics, animation, or photorealism. No Blender render or production process was touched.

The synthetic video probe extracts red moving-object centroids from frame *pixels*. On seven motion scenarios it classified **five of seven**: it detected an injected jump and an injected freeze, but a legitimate brief hold was falsely marked frozen and an intentional camera cut was falsely marked a jump. Neither raw frame differencing nor object motion can infer editorial intent alone. The valid color-change-only hard cut registered a large whole-frame pixel change but was correctly passed by the marker trajectory; it demonstrates why naive frame-difference spikes are not a safe discontinuity gate.

For real completed production-like media, the read-only real-asset probe decoded **180 of 842** frames at low resolution from the October fourteenth completed full-HD forklift motion video, in two **90-frame** contiguous windows near the start and middle. At 30 fps it observed **no exact duplicate frame pairs** and **no frame-difference outliers** by its preset robust-IQR heuristic in those windows. Real-source read-only decoding and sparse optical flow took approximately **30 seconds of CPU time** on busy Nitro. The flow median was near zero for this low-resolution/sample segment; this is not independent motion truth. It does **not** prove smooth animation across the whole video, and no human-annotated true stutter examples were measured. Footage outside the sampled windows, audio synchronization, timeline timestamps and final publication encoding remain unexamined.

## Analyzer registry decision and hardware

The task-appropriate **provisional engineering order** is: for instrumented UI, use actual rendered DOM/accessibility geometry, scroll/clipping bounds, computed-color contrast and explicit intended text, backed by screenshot evidence; for screenshot-only content, use OCR-with-region and pixel segmentation plus an independently specified text/layout contract, escalating uncertain visual cases rather than issuing a clean pass; for diagrams compare expected connection/topology and text labels against a rendered reference and use pixel extraction only for visibly testable components; for mechanics use actual mesh topology, world-space transforms/collisions, normal/depth buffers, scale and reference multiview renders before learned visual/photorealism judgment; for video use timestamps, optical flow/feature tracks, temporal trajectories and cut-aware validity conditions, followed by true video-level visual review. This is a design direction, not proof that each component is deployed or adequate.

Potential no-download research candidates from vendor/model owners, **all unbenchmarked locally here**: Qwen3-VL 2B/4B models (official model family https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL ), Florence-2 for OCR with regions/grounded object proposals (https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Florence-2-large ), SmolVLM2 2.2B for multimodal video/frame reasoning (https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolVLM2-2.2B-Instruct ), PaddleOCR PP-OCRv6 tiny/small/medium or PP-OCRv5 mobile for dedicated OCR (https://www.paddleocr.ai/main/en/version3.x/module_usage/text_recognition.html ), and standard OpenCV Lucas–Kanade flow (https://docs.opencv.org/doc/doxygen/html/d4/dee/tutorial_optical_flow.html ). Vendor benchmark scores must **not** be confused with accuracy on this UI/mechanics corpus. Model VRAM requirements and installed-runtime compatibility were **not** measured. No downloads or model load performed.

At the beginning of this continuation Nitro's six-gigabyte RTX 3060 used over five gigabytes of VRAM, while production Blender, an FFmpeg renderer/encoder and several unrelated CPU processes were active. CPU memory and disk had capacity for bounded work. GPU tests were not safe under the remaining margin, and Thor's user-facing Coder/Qwen3.8 and other research workloads were protected. No model service was interrupted, no production render restarted, and no heavy 3D scene rendered.
