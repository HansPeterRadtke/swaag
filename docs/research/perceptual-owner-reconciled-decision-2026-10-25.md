## Settled answer and immediately usable defaults

**GUI/contrast.** With a real instrumented GUI, start with actual element `clientWidth` versus `scrollWidth`, computed overflow and clipped ancestors, actual browser rendering at supported breakpoints/font scales, required text and W3C accessibility tests through `axe-core`. Only judge narrow properties defined by the source contract. In the earlier W3C ACT evaluation, the pinned axe-core version correctly classified twenty-three determinate source cases among thirty-two executed, misclassified one applicability case and returned eight inconclusive outcomes. Two original script-bearing source cases were deliberately excluded. A failed concrete contrast rule is useful; passing a subset is not full WCAG certification, and gradients/complex backgrounds can remain unknown. In instrumented real Firefox sites, nine known target clipping containers were detected with no false accusations on nine controls; however, the root-viewport-only rule missed five of seven protrusions and knowing the exact target element was required.

**Screenshot-only GUI.** Do not approve based on generic CLIP, Qwen3-VL or ScreenVLM prose. The earlier Thor Qwen3-VL eight-billion-parameter screenshot pilot reportedly passed three of four and **missed a clipped-text defect**. The previously celebrated six of six checks were synthetic source-metadata geometry and are not evidence of correct image perception. The raw original four-case runtime file is not currently present at its old Nitro path, so this is a *preserved source-linked earlier experimental finding*, not an independently repeated measurement today. ScreenParser YOLO produced many element boxes on thirty-two real Firefox site images, but only eleven matched the one independently selected DOM target at intersection-over-union at least one half, averaging roughly sixty-three seconds of CPU per image. That measures narrow target localization with an imperfect source-vs-pixel box oracle, **not general element mAP or clipping classifier accuracy**. Tesseract OCR can recover expected text; absent text or pixel differences should raise *review needed*, never an unqualified layout acceptance.

**Actual independently human-reviewed UI-like imagery.** SlideAudit contains human ratings by three annotators per rendered presentation slide. Its screenshot-only cut-off OCR detector found **fifty-eight of eighty-two** defects, missed **twenty-four**, and falsely flagged **twenty-six of eighty-two** category-negative controls. Source rectangle overlap found **ninety-three of one hundred and five** annotated occlusions, missed **twelve** and flagged **thirty-three of one hundred and five** controls. Low-contrast glyph-patch proxy found **eight of twenty** positives with **zero flags among twenty controls**, too small to infer very low global false-alarm probability; that grayscale patch score is NOT actual foreground/background WCAG contrast. These are real slide renderings (some deliberately altered), **not end-user native or web applications**. Separately, paired OCR on **ninety-seven other-app Scry GUI screenshots** detected twenty-one of twenty-eight source-tagged typography/missing-content cases and flagged thirty-four of sixty-nine cases lacking those tags; the negatives are not certified fully defect-free. No tested screenshot-only approach safely passes unattended final GUI release.

**Diagrams.** Authoritative graph/entity/edge/source data are the strongest basis for checking required nodes, arrows, directions and labels. Exactly nine hundred and twenty-four numeric references from one hundred and fifty-four real original SADU software diagrams were internally consistent with their manual source graph. This validates those source properties, **not** the rasterized arrow and text appearance. Eleven existing VLM model runs were independently re-scored against the original diagrams' question references: the strongest achieved **seventy-two percent** strict exact QA, while Tesseract recovered **eight hundred and twenty of one thousand seven hundred and thirty-three** expected distinct entity labels. Even the leading vision model produced many spurious and missing labels. Use a source graph for known topology, OCR for expected visible text and specialized image/line checking as evidence; unresolved links and human-meaning issues require independent visual review.

**Physical/three-dimensional industrial objects.** The safest acceptance component checks named expected parts, world-space meshes, units, triangle/physics collisions, support chains, joints and allowable movements; actual rendered images must then be inspected separately for appearance. On real consecutive warehouse scene versions, **all seventy-two bars touched their pallets but none touched the intended original racks at both ends** in the intermediate stage. After changing all twelve rack orientations, **all seventy-two bars touched the correct racks at both ends**, with the pallet meshes and bearing-bar geometry unchanged. That is a verified contact property in one correlated source design, not structural strength, welding/material certification or visual photorealism. A distinct external generated-three-dimensional benchmark with two-rater human majority source labels found that a crude source AABB-overlap signal detected **nine of thirty-three** missing components, missed twenty-four and falsely flagged twenty of eighty-six negatives. A generic VLM or broad mesh validity result cannot detect semantic missing machine components reliably. Industrial image anomaly models such as PatchCore and EfficientAD remain useful *research candidates* for trained specific products, not locally qualified forklift completeness or mechanical-physics inspectors.

**Video and audiovisual.** Start with exact recorded source identity, permitted editor-approved cuts/holds, native PTS/frame rates and audio alignment to actual recorded events, then compare decoded frames to the approved reference where available. Frame differencing, FFmpeg `freezedetect`, pHash and optical flow propose suspect times but cannot know an intentional static hold or a physically frozen character. On thirty-eight actual edited source videos in Adobe VideoSham, FFmpeg detected **seven of nineteen** temporal author edits and falsely flagged **twelve of nineteen** other edited controls. An original-footage pHash reference method was sensitive to changes but falsely accused many unrelated edits and abstained on uncertain matches. Video duration alone classified the author-manipulation labels more effectively, exposing a construction shortcut rather than a visual quality capability. Across fifteen hundred in-engine controlled Godot source videos, twelve published VLMs had **at best about fifty-four percent author-label accuracy**, with strong positive-vs-negative response bias. These published models were not retested on Nitro or Thor. The two actual warehouse/dock full-HD encoded files had regular 30-frame-per-second PTS and four hundred and twenty decoded frames apiece; this verifies only timebase integrity, not visual smoothness, and the high-res tail-framehash result remains unverified after its denied retrieval. Original test recording also requires verifying correct capture and audio-to-visual synchronization in real interactions; a silent video or correct timestamps alone cannot satisfy that.

## Decision gate for work that can be used immediately

**First**, for a precise source-defined property, use a deterministic source check and require explicit PASS, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE; meaningful violations can stop that specific output. Examples include known clipping ancestors, missing required graph nodes, wrong authored CAD support contact and unexpected gaps in an approved edit timeline. **Second**, analyze the *actual final pixels or audio* with the appropriately tested OCR, vision parser, image difference or event detector, and return candidate region or time plus uncertainty, not a universal accept/reject. **Third**, for design meaning, suspected unintended object overlap, nuanced contrast on pictures, load-bearing engineering, photorealism, animation physics and editorial motion intent, compare to verified independent source reference and obtain qualified human/physical adjudication where no calibrated tool exists. A PASS from one tier **never** silently approves the others. Unknown means review needed, not green. Check actual screen/video at the user's supported display size and font scaling, not only internal data or screenshots of a convenient state.

These choices were grounded in current reports and original recording. On busy Nitro the six-gigabyte RTX 3060 has under two gigabytes free and other 3D/render/voice/trading jobs are active, so no new GPU vision-model loads were attempted. Specialist `UI-Lens` (CVPR 2026) has highly relevant real mobile GUI source annotations for clipping and overlap, but its current Hugging Face release is **auto-gated**; no authorization was used or bypassed and no local benchmark claim can be made. The preceding ScreenVLM four-crop, ScreenParser thirty-two-site and earlier source-boundary cases are not adequate substitutes for a fresh truly human-labeled final GUI release set.
