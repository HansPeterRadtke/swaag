## Dataset A — real archived websites with originally offscreen defects, now captured in view

A previous UIJudgeBench v0.4.0 experiment revealed the essential benchmark design error: twelve of sixteen externally labeled layout anomalies were **entirely outside** the supplied fixed PNGs. A model cannot see an offscreen defect. This continuation generated **32 new Firefox screenshots** from archived static HTML of authentic web pages: sixteen confirmed good controls and sixteen browser-verified faulty variants (nine text-container clipping, seven viewport-protrusion). In each case, the target CSS selector was scrolled into the viewport and a genuine browser image of its local region was saved. Each target rectangle intersects the captured viewport; original and new screenshots are separate, SHA-verified artifacts, not replays or rewritten labels. Inputs are preserved outside Git in `/data/var/perceptual-independent-20261024/visible-ui-real` with target positions, HTML source hashes and source text kept separate from image-only model inputs.

**Crucial qualification:** the *target rectangle* being visible does not guarantee that every distant horizontal overflow defect is visually decidable from a cropped image; it is still impossible to infer whether the page should extend farther without expected layout or full viewport evidence. This corpus solves the gross *offscreen target* failure but does not magically solve all unknown-intent cases. For the dedicated image-only probe, **only image pixels** reached the CLIP encoder, never source selectors, author labels or expected text. The fixed pre-run zero-shot prompts described intact GUI layout versus cut-off/overlapping/out-of-window controls. Pretrained `openai/clip-vit-base-patch32`, fp32 CPU, approximately 24 seconds for all 32 screenshots. On dev examples: text clipping TP **3/7**, FP **2/7**; viewport extension TP **2/6**, FP **2/6**. On the previously external-separated test examples, it missed **all three actual defects** (two clipped, one viewport extension) while accepting all three controls. This is a **negative image-only general-model result**, not proof that another UI-specialized model fails similarly.

## Dataset B — full human-reviewed real/generated mobile-app screenshot pairs

**Primary external source:** Scry Design Diff Eval, researcher Ejiro Pinnock (2026): <https://huggingface.co/datasets/Scrymore/scry-design-diff-eval>, detailed scoring method <https://blog.scrymore.com/paper/>. The entire publicly available release was acquired: **sixteen Parquet shards, approximately 198.7 megabytes, 311 reference-versus-implemented mobile-app screenshot pairs** from **52 distinct app groups**, with **557 human-labeled issue boxes and defect categories**. Of these, **234** pairs have known tagged problems and **77** have no scored annotations. The latter were **not exhaustively certified clean**; flag rates on those controls are a useful operational diagnostic but are not mathematically proven false-positive rates. Original app screenshot rights remain with their owners; screenshots and original third-party data stay outside Git with source SHA receipts.

The evaluator was intentionally separated into two phases. Phase one read **only images, IDs and app group**, calculated resized RGB mean absolute pixel difference, grayscale structural difference (`1−SSIM`), and simple local changed-region proposals; persisted every raw prediction **before opening any human labels**. Phase two opened the labels and selected thresholds on a predefined app-grouped calibration fold: stable hash of `app_slug` modulo four selected fourteen app groups comprising **62** pairs, with **249** heldout pairs from **38 completely different app groups**. That is stronger than random per-screenshot splitting because screens from the same app stay together. Both MAE and SSIM used a fixed calibration grid, maximizing balanced accuracy among the calibration no-tagged/tagged-screen proxies. SSIM was selected on calibration alone, not on test results. Neither method attempts to identify semantic defect categories.

### Measured screen-level defect-presence results (human known-tagged vs no-tagged proxy)

| Fixed candidate and frozen threshold | Heldout tagged positives found | Heldout positives missed | No-tagged controls flagged | No-tagged controls accepted | Heldout balanced accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RGB mean absolute pixel difference, threshold **0.06** | **145/186** | **41** | **38/63** | **25/63** | **58.8%** |
| Structural difference `1−SSIM`, threshold **0.26** | **157/186** | **29** | **41/63** | **22/63** | **59.7%** |

The SSIM method had **84.4%** tagged-screen sensitivity, but flagged **65.1%** of the no-tagged control pairs. The grouped bootstrap confidence interval for its heldout balanced accuracy was approximately **47.9–67.1%**, and the MAE comparator **46.6–67.0%**. Its calibration balanced accuracy was **79.5%**, dropping markedly on different apps; calibration performance must **not** be cited as evidence of deployment reliability. Both methods compare paired references and implementations; they are not a screenshot-only answer when there is no matching intended reference. Resizing images with different aspect ratios introduces additional differences, and artistic deviations from a design may be intentional. Since no-tagged pairs are not truly certified error-free, the reported specificity and precision are labelled *proxy diagnostics*, not independent real-world false-positive estimates.

### Precise defect localization remains weak

A single thresholded RGB-difference heatmap with connected-component rectangles, fixed before human labels were read, produced **4,138 candidate boxes on the 249 heldout screens**. At a **0.10 box-IoU** threshold against the human bounding boxes, it geometrically overlapped **253 of 458 known human issue boxes (55.2%)**. However, the heatmap proposed an average of **14.6 boxes for each no-tagged control screen** and at least one box for **93.7%** of those screens. **This is spatial overlap without *any* defect-class prediction**. Consequently, its fifty-five-percent value cannot be compared to the benchmark's official issue recall requiring both a matching human defect tag **and** location. The result is a typical high-noise attention map, not an intelligent defect report or a viable gate without substantial further filtering.

**Relevant published genuine multimodal model benchmark (source author's independent tests, not reproduced here):** on this **same 311-pair** release the best tested frontier model configuration, Claude Fable 5, recovered **40.4%** of human-tagged *tag-and-box* issues and flagged **92.2%** of the no-tagged controls; Kimi K2.7 Code 38.2%/100%, Gemini 3.5 Flash 37.5%/96.1%, and Codex GPT-5.5 xhigh 37.3%/98.7%. These are individual author-published model-plus-harness outcomes, not the same metric as our simple binary change detection. Best typography issue recall was only **20%**. The benchmark is much more realistic evidence than the former four-case Thor test, but still limits transfer: mobile screenshot *paired diffs* are not the same as single-screen clipping/contrast, entire interactive behavior, or live 3D/video quality. See <https://blog.scrymore.com/leaderboard/> and <https://blog.scrymore.com/blog/introducing-scry-design-diff-eval/>.

## Dataset C — W3C ACT official minimum text contrast cases

**Independent source:** official W3C Accessibility Conformance Testing rule **afw4f7, Text has minimum contrast** (<https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/afw4f7/>), under WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.3 (<https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/>). All **34 official cases** were downloaded with source hashes, comprising **12 passed, 11 failed and 11 inapplicable** externally authored outcomes. A completely separate *instrumented browser* evaluator implemented the WCAG 4.5-to-one contrast formula for ordinary text and 3-to-one for large or bold text, using computed Firefox CSS colors; it intentionally abstained on gradients, background images, transparency/background compositing or shadows it could not reliably model. Disabled/hidden/decoration exceptions were attempted; network and page JavaScript were disabled. This is an independent small normative test for a flat-color rule, **not screenshot-only pixel contrast nor certification as a full W3C ACT/axe-core implementation**.

The new checker produced **five correct detected failures**, **six correct passes**, **one false failure**, **eleven undecided/applicability-abstaining cases** among pass/fail expectations, **seven correctly identified inapplicable cases and four incorrect inapplicable determinations**. The detailed three-way confusion matrix is retained verbatim. Critically, an apparently perfect **five of five evaluated failed** sample with no false negatives is misleading because **six other genuinely failed cases were not successfully classified**. The appropriate pass/fail outcome must include undecided cases in coverage. The test establishes that simple solid-color CSS contrasts are tractable but real accessibility behavior needs knowledge of background images, transparency, parent opacity, text shadows, computed backgrounds, hidden/disabled/decorative language, and incomplete static rendering; use a standards-tested browser accessibility engine and explicitly report cases it cannot adjudicate. Do not promote the custom small checker to production.

## Research-supported analyzer registry and what remains unproven

For DOM-backed applications, computed geometry and actual browser overflow measurements *plus the relevant containment/ancestor stack*, CSS color and documented WCAG thresholds, accessibility properties and actual screenshots are the most directly testable instruments. For screenshot-only UI, a **screen parser** such as **ScreenVLM** (<https://huggingface.co/docling-project/ScreenVLM>, ICML ScreenParse 2026) can provide boxes/element classes and OCR where needed; unlike the CLIP experiment, that model is specifically trained on screen elements, but **not locally measured yet**, and its boxes are not themselves evidence that layout is *correct*. For semantic GUI user-experience judgment, UI-UX (<https://huggingface.co/afx-team/UI-UX>, UXBench) is task-specific but its author-reported ~79.6% UXBench score is not a failure-free guarantee and not our local accuracy. For diagrams, retain authoritative graph/chart/source topology and independently validate rendered labels and encodings; general VLM diagram-quiz results vary substantially and are not an overlap gate. For industrial imagery, a truly target-trained normal-domain PatchCore/EfficientAD may be worth evaluating, but physical correctness of actual moving machines requires scene units, collision/contact/joint constraints and human/independent industrial reference review; **no locally validated general mechanical render judge**. For video, use actual frame timestamps, matched final-edit reference, content-aware optical flow and verified cut/hold semantics; CGVQM and specialist temporal models must be calibrated with external frame-level truth and separately reviewed rendering-quality labels before autonomously accepting a final product.

**Open research:** independently labeled actual overlapping/low-contrast GUI screenshot pairs not just measured full-page accessibility labels; compare a genuinely UI-specialized ScreenVLM or UX judge with the frozen 32-image visible-localized corpus and Scry full reference corpus on a proven idle machine; instrumented 3D machine contact/appearance datasets with independently physically verified correct and incorrect real renders; real *naturally occurring* video stutter/tearing/physically implausible sequences with confirmed frame/audio timelines and intentional-edit controls. High-quality candidate names and favorable author's paper numbers are **not** equivalent to verified accuracy on this owner's product. Accuracy claim remains **PARTIAL**, not COMPLETE.
