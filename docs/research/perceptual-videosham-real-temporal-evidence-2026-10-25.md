# Video perceptual analyzer research: original third-party editor-labeled temporal manipulations

**Source-tested 25 October 2026; research state PARTIAL.** This experiment addresses the main previously missing empirical control: no-reference image frame-change and source-reference matching must be tested on *real videos edited by others*, rather than only unit fixtures and intentional local frame copying. Source [Adobe VideoSham](https://github.com/adobe-research/VideoSham-dataset), original authored video-manipulation dataset and exact released original-vs-processed MP4 pairs, research license **Adobe Research License version 1.1a, noncommercial**. No original video, thumbnails or human free-text editor instructions were committed to the SWAAG source project.

The public first archive contains **nineteen author-assigned frame duplication, dropping, reversal and speed-change videos**. Before video content analysis, all nineteen were selected along with **nineteen different source-video families bearing only other author-assigned edits**, stratified across audio, text, added/removed objects and background/color changes. Each selected manipulated file is paired with its own authentic unedited Vimeo footage. All **seventy-six** individual source MP4s were extracted via byte-range ZIP access, verified against original published member CRC32 and exact source byte counts, and independently hashed. The original archive is over two gigabytes; approximately **684.89 megabytes** of selected media were actually required, stored under an isolated research data directory. Selection did **not** examine video features, labels were only joined after the predictor outputs froze, and no production Nitro video/render was touched.

## Direct original author-edit positives, false control flags and abstentions

Source labels are author instructions to make edits, not independent human subjective verdicts about objectionable stutter or overall production quality. The nineteen **other processed edits** are important: they are not simply identical unedited clips, so a detector that flags every encoded or altered clip will accumulate false alarms. The methods and thresholds below were declared before scoring: no-reference near-identical frame runs bracketed by motion; no-reference adjacent frame-change spikes; their OR; frame-wise 64-bit DCT perceptual-hash matching against the exact *related unedited source video*, where less than one-quarter confident matching abstains; and a clearly marked **duration-only metadata confound**. All video pixels were actually decoded at native FPS, then resized to 128 by 72 grayscale for analysis; no GPU or learned VLM was used.

**Direct externally authored reference-control result:** the fixed no-reference motion/hold OR signal detected **2 of 18 scorable** temporal editor cases, with **2 false alerts of 19** other-edited source controls, and had one predeclared decode-limit abstention. Official FFmpeg `freezedetect` at **minus 50 decibels and minimum 0.12 seconds** detected **7 of 19** temporal edits, but falsely flagged **12 of 19** non-temporal edited controls; its event-count ROC AUC was approximately **0.454**. This cannot pass a general freeze/judder release gate. The DCT perceptual-source matching method identified **17 of 18 scorable** temporal edits but falsely flagged **9 of 13** scorable controls, with **one positive and six other-control abstentions**; its conditionally computed AUROC around **0.915** reflects high sensitivity to original-frame deviation, not selectivity for undesired temporal errors. The original native **video duration ratio alone** identified **15 of 18** edit cases with **no false control flags** and an ROC AUC about **0.956**. This duration advantage is a **construction confound**, not a measured visual capability.

The companion `perceptual-videosham-real-temporal-editor-control-2026-10-25.json` retains the exact TP, FP, FN and TN and predeclared continuous-score AUROC, source-type subgroup counts, decode and reference-match abstentions, and source-clip bootstrap/exact binomial intervals. An AUROC score merely ranks the *author's editorial manipulation type* and does not imply that a video visually failed or passed review. Under a sample of nineteen positive and nineteen other edits, uncertainty is substantial; this cannot justify one-hundred-percent safety even for a method with no observed false alarms. Do **not** conflate generator-sourced changes, source encode/resolution artifacts or slow artistic edits with unintended rendering playback faults.

## What changes the immediately usable visual testing default?

When the original footage, approved final frame sequence and allowed holds/cuts are known, a reference-aware temporal check is semantically stronger than a generic detector that simply notices static or abrupt frames. But a frame-level reference must be checked for source identity, FPS and content matching; if a video has been cropped, re-encoded or substantially changed, the detector can and should **abstain**. For screenshot-only video, a frame-difference/flow/freeze check can nominate time intervals for actual visual inspection but cannot infer whether the freeze is editorial or unauthorized. Duration/fps alone can predict an author speed-change task but is categorically not perceptual evidence.

The owner's wider established evidence remains: exact finished warehouse and dock encoded timestamps can be correct while the warehouse has an intentional still-camera ending; Spotlight low-level motion peak detection often approaches the random-time baseline on real annotated synthetic-generation motion faults; photoindustrial CLIP and coarse mesh overlap fail semantic missing-part detection; GUI DOM client/scroll and real source rectangle geometry can prove narrow layout properties while image-only CLIP and OCR return high false alarm rates; the best archived source-diagram VLM answers under three-quarters of true structure questions. Thus a multi-tier toolset is justified, but no universal image/video model currently tested is safe as the release gate.

**Outstanding decisive test:** naturally occurring captured/rendered video playback stalls or undesired camera judder, paired with genuinely clean same-fps/codec controls and independent human frame-accurate labels. [LIVE Mobile Stall II](https://www.colorado.edu/lab/live/live-public-domain-subjective-mobile-stall-video-database-ii) and original LIVE frame-freeze recordings provide higher-quality human subjective data, but their access path requires a form and was not silently used. The CSVTED public [source dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CardiffVisualComputing/CSVTED) includes real source recordings with author inserted, deleted or duplicated frames, but the total release is around forty-four gigabytes and was not needed here. Neither this editorial-control benchmark nor its fixed codec/fps can substitute for naturally occurring unplanned faults. The previously denied other-video result retrievals remain denied, with no retry or workaround.
