# Independent real diagram connector detection and direction — new TopoBench source test

**25 October 2026. Owning project: SWAAG visual perception research. Status: PARTIAL.** The original owner question asks which analyzers can correctly judge *actual* diagram images, including arrows and missing/incorrect connections. Earlier SADU research found source graph count checks can be internally consistent while source-linked image OCR finds under half of intended labels; even the best archived VLM had about seventy-two percent exact structural QA accuracy. This distinct continuation uses the external [TopoBench-180 curated topology benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/WayneGuo0011/TopoBench-180), pinned at the original repository revision in the accompanying evidence JSON, with **sixty-nine actual redistributable real diagrams** and their separately released source node/edge annotations. The original test archive has one hundred and sixty-two entries but ninety-three image files are *link-only* and have unresolved redistribution permission; they were **not** retrieved or scored. The sixty-nine originals comprise fifty-four AI2D drawings and fifteen source-licensed network diagrams. Original source pixels, model outputs and external annotations remain under isolated `/data/var`, not in Git. The external graph labels are curated source annotations and not a new independent blinded human re-annotation.

## Before looking at the source graph relationships: true image-only extraction

On the actual image pixels only, predeclared CPU Tesseract **PSM eleven and PSM six**, and Canny plus Hough line segments, were extracted for **all sixty-nine images**, with no source graph names or directed edges passed to the extraction stage. The run was pinned to its dataset revision and actual source parquet hash. The first extractor took about **209 seconds** wall time on busy Nitro with one low-priority CPU thread and about **265 MiB** peak RSS; no GPU or model inference. The outputs were frozen before connecting to original source graph labels. Matching a trusted source node name to a uniquely recognized OCR token sequence was permitted only in the later source-assisted scoring step; multiple or missing OCR matches **abstain**. Then a fixed pixel-line alignment score was compared to each expected annotated connection or source-annotated non-edge between uniquely grounded node pairs. A nearest-neighbor layout-only detector, and a trivial no-link policy, were predeclared controls, not tuned after labels.

## External-source true connections versus annotated non-connections

The external images contain **seven hundred and forty source-annotated nodes** and **nine hundred and sixty-six unique undirected connection pairs** (direction handled separately). OCR PSM eleven uniquely grounded just **two hundred and twenty-nine nodes**, with only **thirty-eight of sixty-nine diagrams** providing any scorable grounded node pairs. That left only **one hundred and ninety-eight of nine hundred and sixty-six original annotated connections** with both endpoints located. The other source links are UNKNOWN due to absent or ambiguous OCR grounding, not confirmed no-edges.

Among the **six hundred and sixty-nine conditional node pairs actually scorable** (one hundred and ninety-eight reference edges and four hundred and seventy-one source-negative non-edges), the fixed real image line detector achieved **one hundred and fifty-one true positives, one hundred and fifty-eight false positives, forty-seven false negatives and three hundred and thirteen true negatives**. Recall **76.3 percent**, specificity **66.5 percent**, precision only **48.9 percent** and balanced accuracy **71.4 percent**. Its continuous line-alignment score ranked annotated edges above non-edges with ROC area **0.783**, with **ninety-five percent source-diagram clustered bootstrap interval [0.7423, 0.8249]**. These are conditional on successfully located nodes and source annotation completeness, **not end-to-end diagram acceptance reliability**.

The same-budget spatial nearest-neighbor baseline achieved **ninety-three true positives, fifty-five false positives, one hundred and five misses and four hundred and sixteen correct negatives** on those exact pairs, or **67.6 percent** balanced accuracy. Thus the line-pixel evidence adds a useful ranking signal: the diagram-grouped bootstrap interval for line-score AUROC minus nearest-neighbor spatial score is **[0.0492, 0.1558]**. But this is not accurate enough to reject a diagram automatically, because nearly half the detected connection proposals are false according to source references, and most original links were never OCR grounded. The unconditional always-no-edge baseline has **zero false positives but misses every original connection**, illustrating why specificity alone can be misleading. The independent PSM six setting grounded **one hundred and seventy-three nodes** and found **one hundred and two of one hundred and forty scorable source edges** but falsely flagged **one hundred and twenty-four of three hundred and thirty-four annotated nonedges**; it was not silently selected or pooled with PSM eleven.

## Stronger spatial-distance-only control, added after viewing the original results

The first predeclared nearest-neighbor comparison used a binary nearest-node flag; this might be an unusually weak geometry-only baseline. To avoid over-crediting Hough line pixels, we carried out a **separate explicitly POST-HOC diagnostic on the same already consumed benchmark**, without re-running OCR, changing source images or fitting new thresholds. On each previously grounded node pair, the continuous *negative Euclidean distance divided by image diagonal* was computed from the original frozen source-blind OCR anchor positions. It contains **zero connector line pixels**. The test samples and positives/negatives are exactly the same.

For PSM eleven, the distance-only continuous score achieved **ROC AUC 0.726**, compared with **0.783** for the frozen pixel-line score. The paired source-diagram clustered bootstrap interval for the **+0.057 Hough-minus-distance AUC** is approximately **+0.002 to +0.116**. For PSM six, continuous distance reached **0.701**, versus **0.760** for Hough, and the paired interval **−0.028 to +0.150 includes zero**. Therefore the stronger same-budget source-proximity control **does not eliminate all observed line-pixel signal**, but makes its advantage **small, uncertain across OCR settings and insufficient for a reliable automatic diagram judge**. This diagnostic was selected after seeing the first control and must not be presented as a preregistered independent supertest or general claim of improved graph parsing. Frozen original OCR+Hough and the external source labels remain unchanged; the exact new source hash/metrics are linked from the compact evidence JSON.

## Arrow directions remain genuinely unresolved

A Hough line has no intrinsic arrow direction. To explicitly avoid presenting undirected line hits as correct directed topology, the experimental scorer reported a *direction-of-reading bias-only baseline*: if the image connector proposal exists, guess the arrow points from left to right or top to bottom, **without any arrowhead evidence**. On detected source pairs with exactly one annotated direction, this guessed the source orientation correctly **eighty of one hundred and forty-seven** times with PSM eleven (approximately fifty-four percent), and forty-four of one hundred with PSM six. The primary direction result is consistent with chance. The scorer does not silently count reciprocal source arrows as a single directed edge. The correct practical default is to verify directed edges from authoritative source graph/data, and require visible arrowhead/connector inspection independently when image direction could differ. OCR/Hough is a candidate locator, not a pixel-to-directed-graph model.

## Scope and operating decision

This is one **genuinely new public dataset** with unmodified original diagrams, original source graph positive and negative pairs, a source-blind pixel first stage, two predefined OCR variants and a same-budget layout-only baseline. The original dataset's graph annotations, although curated and useful reference truth, are not independently certified to cover every visible arrow and connector; original source license status is recorded for each category in the local manifest. The deliberately different AI2D and network diagram families make the OCR coverage gap significant. All links whose labels cannot be grounded remain **UNKNOWN**, not passes. An actual source-aware diagram verifier can enforce the required directed source graph and ask the image analysis to propose potential visible connections, but it must not issue a final automatic pass without verified rendered arrowhead direction and text/connector visibility. This does not invalidate existing GUI geometry and axe-core narrow checks, the owner's verified warehouse geometry chain, the source-based video time checks or the prior negative vision model findings. It refines the recommendation: **diagram pixel-line geometry is useful for finding potential connections, while direction, graph completeness and final visual semantics need a stronger oracle**.

**Most decisive remaining technical gap:** independently human-verified drawn arrowhead orientation and absence-of-unwanted-connectors across new diagram types, plus production GUI truly clean controls, unrelated physically certified industrial scenes/renderings and time-coded natural video faults. No production services, GPU models, source files, calendar events, earlier denied results or uncertain Calendar sends were touched. General visual approval remains PARTIAL.
