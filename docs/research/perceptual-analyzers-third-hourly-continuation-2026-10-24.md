## New independently scored five-VLM ensemble on genuine human 3D defects

**Source:** Zhao et al., [3D-DefectBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10826), pinned release [`aieval2026/3d-defectbench`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/aieval2026/3d-defectbench) revision `94a1ce5b7fe4b46e0388b2593d4a6f3cd3421b77`. The five human-evaluated VLM outputs are **published original model predictions**, not new model inference calls on Nitro: Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4.1, GPT-5 and Qwen3-VL 235B. The previous pass matched their exact common eight-view binary per-aspect prompt/render protocol against ninety-nine expert-adjudicated generated GLB assets, thirty-one expert-labeled missing-parts defects. Source rater agreement on missing parts was only about **76.8%** over the ninety-nine doubly rated assets, an important independent truth limitation.

This pass added **predeclared simple five-judge voting policies**, not a tuned or trained neural ensemble: report missing parts if any one, at least three, at least four or all five models report it. All four policies, including unfavorable results, are saved in `3d-defectbench-2026-paper/expert_5vlm_fixed_vote_ensemble_20261024.json`; reproduction `tests/research/three_d_human_vlm_ensemble_20261024.py`. On ninety-nine expert assets with thirty-one known missing-parts positives:

| Fixed VLM vote rule | TP | FP | FN | TN | Recall | Precision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Any one of five flags a problem | 20 | 29 | 11 | 39 | 64.5% | 40.8% |
| Majority three of five | 8 | 8 | 23 | 60 | 25.8% | 50.0% |
| Four of five | 4 | 5 | 27 | 63 | 12.9% | 44.4% |
| All five | 1 | 3 | 30 | 65 | 3.2% | 25.0% |

On the *smaller mechanical* expert subcategory, **nine** positives in **thirty** assets: majority flags only **one** defect, with **two** false alarms; any-one flags **five**, with **nine** false alarms. The results rule out naïve model-vote agreement as a dependable way to fix the very large false-negative and false-positive problems. The 99 outputs comprise only fifty independent-ish source prompt groups; previous expert-score uncertainty intervals are prompt-cluster bootstrapped, not 99 independent industrial manufacturers. The task is *human-perceived missing parts of generated shapes*, not actual engineering load ratings, robot constraints, kinematics or fully measured real forklift tolerances.

## New actual GLB structure checks versus those human labels

**Source:** ten original golden-set 3D-DefectBench machine-like GLBs, selected beforehand to include four externally majority-labeled “missing parts” and six “not missing parts” examples, not a random sample. Actual GLB mesh geometry was parsed CPU-only using isolated existing `trimesh`; no render was run, no source asset edited. Fixed numerical predicates: finite vertex coordinates, open/nonmanifold mesh, degenerate face area, and crude submesh axis-aligned bounding-box overlap above two percent. All ten meshes had finite vertices and no triangle with near-zero area at the chosen relative tolerance. **Every mesh had an open/nonwatertight submesh**, including all six human-no-missing-part controls. A global reject-on-open-mesh policy therefore has six false alarms. The AABB-overlap indicator happened to flag four of four missing-parts samples but also **three of six** no-missing-parts controls; it is not an intersection or physical collision test, and the tiny purposively selected corpus does **not** establish recall reliability. Valid triangles never prove a motor, door, loader or linkage actually exists. All independent results in `3d-defectbench/mesh_structure_vs_human_missing_parts_20261024.json`, reproduced by `tests/research/real_mechanical_mesh_invariants_20261024.py`.

**Engineering meaning:** exact scene coordinates/units, intentionally supported contact surfaces, triangle collision/penetration under known object identities, constraint/joint kinematics, normal and depth passes and measured industrial designs remain the only good route to *proving specifically encoded physical properties*. A GLB-only broadphase or a generic visual LLM cannot infer everything that a semantically missing part would have done. More real rendering-and-physics reference work is outstanding.

## Reassessment of earlier apparently successful optical-flow physical-video benchmark

**Source:** [Rapidata Image-to-Video Physics Human Feedback](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Rapidata/image-to-video-physics-human-feedback-benchmark), source revision `0adbb8421595188087df72ab2da72129c71ea227`. Human preference aggregates and twenty-four exact original five-second MP4s (twelve real recorded continuations versus Cosmos 2.5 generations) were already captured in the preceding same-chat continuation. Both video streams were decoded and normalized to twelve frames per second before frozen CPU optical-flow, frame difference and flow-acceleration measurements. The human aggregates favored real footage in **all twelve** selected scenarios; flow acceleration agreed in **ten of twelve**, motion-compensated residual in **nine of twelve**, and simple second-frame difference in **three of twelve**. These are preferences, not frame-accurate motion-defect labels.

This pass closed the confounding hypothesis using a metadata-only negative control on the **same original video pairs**, plus a second public same-prompt **six** Cosmos 2.5 vs MiniMax H3 generated-video pair set. The fixed motion metrics on the latter agreed with human preference in **six of six** for flow acceleration and **four of six** for warp residual. However, **native frame rate alone agrees with human preference in all eighteen pair comparisons**, because real video is thirty FPS versus generated Cosmos sixteen FPS, and MiniMax generations twenty-four FPS versus Cosmos sixteen FPS. *Downsampling both streams to twelve FPS does not remove motion cadence and codec/generator confounding created before sampling.* Results reproduce under `tests/research/public_video_fps_confound_audit.py`, original receipts `rapidata-physics-video/fps_only_preference_confound_20261024.json`, and frozen video computations. We therefore **withdraw any implied claim that the optical-flow feature judged real physical plausibility**. The earlier archived `12_human_rated_real_vs_ai_temporal_comparison_20261024.json` unfortunately retains a contradictory per-metric Boolean field, `not_independent_of_source_model_fps: false`, despite its own limitations paragraph documenting the source FPS confound. **That Boolean was wrong.** The reproduction script is corrected to `true` here, the historical raw comparison has **not** been rewritten, and the subsequent immutable `fps_only_preference_confound_20261024.json` is the ground-truth audit for this research conclusion. This is an important negative result and explicit correction to the earlier seemingly strong numbers. No clean-vs-defective binary stutter false-positive rate can be inferred from human pairwise votes.

## New independent temporal-error test on actual human-labeled videos, not artificial red squares

**Primary source:** Chinchure et al., [Spotlight, ECCV 2026](https://spotlight-video.github.io/) and pinned original dataset [`UBC-ViL/Spotlight-VideoGen-Errors`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UBC-ViL/Spotlight-VideoGen-Errors) commit `4165ea17ece2fda7b6b0df52bcbf07348a31253e`, license CC BY-NC 4.0. This source contains six hundred source AI-generated videos from Veo 3, Seedance and LTX-2 with **1,604 human time-localized error annotations**, across motion faults, physical violations, appearance/disappearance, prompt adherence, logic and anatomy. The *Spotlight paper authors*, not this local project, report error-localization quality score **0.508 for humans** versus **0.250 Gemini 2.5 Pro zero shot** and **0.148 Qwen3-VL 8B**, evaluated using matching criteria involving temporal and semantic agreement. Model hallucinations and wrongly flagged whole clips are major failure modes. Those are **external source results**, not a new local video VLM run or the same metric as our peak-finding baseline.

To avoid repeating synthetic temporal fault tests, selected exactly **twenty-four original videos** spanning eight distinct *prompt families*, each from all three generators: four prompt families had a human motion-error annotation in at least two of three generated versions, four prompt families had no motion annotation in any generated version. Within each bucket, source prompt IDs were chosen using fixed `SHA256(prompt_id)` ordering, with a twelve-megabyte-per-file cap. This deliberately enriches motion faults: **nine motion-labeled videos, fifteen without a motion label, and eleven human-labeled motion segments**. The negative class means **unlabeled for this particular motion error**, not human-certified perfect video; other types of defects remain. Original media are about 117.5MB, downloaded with source sizes/hashes into `/data/var/perceptual-independent-20261024/spotlight-videogen/`, never committed to Git. Reproduction of the exact selection: `tests/research/spotlight_human_motion_source.py`.

A single-thread CPU processor decoded the videos at **eight FPS, 160×90 grayscale**. The three genuinely image-only signals—optical-flow-magnitude acceleration, motion-compensated frame residual and ordinary frame difference—used fixed Farnebäck settings, a fixed robust outlier threshold, and three prominent local temporal peaks separated by at least 0.375 seconds. Every raw signal and per-clip classification was written to immutable local JSON **before any human annotation was used to score them**. Total measured CPU wall time about **104 seconds**. Fixed anomaly-rule outcomes, compared only with the external *motion* class:

| Image-only temporal rule | Human motion clips flagged (of 9) | Flags on clips with no motion annotation (of 15, **proxy only**) | Annotated motion segments intersecting 3 highest peaks (of 11) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Flow magnitude acceleration | 4 | 4 | 9 |
| Flow-compensated frame residual | 5 | 0 | 9 |
| Raw frame difference | 1 | 0 | 8 |

The seemingly good **9/11** temporal peak overlap is greatly inflated by broad human error intervals; one human motion annotation spans the entire video. A **fixed 10,000-draw random-grid peak control** with identical three-peak spacing hits **6.70/11** annotated events on average, and random peaks already hit **nine or more** in about **10.28%** of the simulations. This is insufficient evidence of robust temporal localization or semantics. The flow-warp residual signal still **missed four of nine** motion-labeled videos using the fixed predeclared anomaly rule; no statistically defensible true false-alarm estimate exists for the fifteen not motion-labeled clips. Source-generator groups share the same prompt and cannot be treated as fully independent observations. Case-by-case feature reports, time segments, generation and source SHA files are in `/data/var` with reproduction scripts `spotlight_human_temporal_flow_eval.py` and `spotlight_motion_chance_and_leakage_audit.py`. The raw peak finder outputs no explanation, artifact class, physics reason or camera-edit intent. This is deliberately **not** counted as an actual working video LLM or production editor checker.

## Task-specific decisions: settle only what independent evidence proves

| Real visual task | Component justified by current evidence | Explicitly unresolved or rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Instrumented GUI clipping/overlap | Actual browser DOM layout, scroll/client extents, element ancestor clipping, screenshot trace and independently expected visible text; previously 9/9 author-confirmed clipped-element cases detected and 0/9 paired clean false alarms **when exact target identity supplied** | Whole-document root scrollWidth missed five of seven viewport protrusions; self-adjusting layouts, shadows and intentional occlusions need real annotated cases. |
| Web text contrast | Real `axe-core 4.14.0` WCAG 1.4.3 checker; W3C ACT cases with explicit pass/fail/inapplicable/unknown, **no unconditional compliance claim** | Some source scripts excluded, incomplete on gradients/images/transparency and other special cases; previous simplistic OCR-local-contrast was severely over-sensitive. |
| Screenshot-only GUI text and layout | OCR with external expected text and benchmarked UI element locators as **evidence**, not a judge; paired SSIM/MAE visual diff only to locate change regions for review | Our ScreenVLM 316M cropped-only v2 trial recovered 0/4 expected labels and took ~273s CPU/screen; Scry 311 human-tagged pairs have 41/63 no-tagged control SSIM flags, not standalone acceptance. |
| Diagrams and charts | Authoritative topology/chart data/TikZ plus independent rendered text/axis/line bounds, and external human diagram QA on hard holdouts | Tesseract found 42/134 simple literal TikZ labels on 37 eligible independent diagrams; no source-independent VLM passed semantic diagram correctness. |
| Generated 3D industrial/mechanical objects | Actual asset-space geometry, scene units, intended CAD component identities, joint/contact/kinematic constraints, and multiview/source-provenance human adjudication | Real 10-GLB test shows open mesh not equal missing parts; expert five-VLM majority 8/31 TP + 8/68 FP, physical realism and photorealism remain unverified. |
| Temporal video defects, physically impossible motion and final encoded output | Frame timestamp/PTS and known approved cut/hold timeline, exact or tolerant matched-reference comparison for a *specified* property, optical flow only as a low-confidence diagnostic | RapData 18/18 FPS confound disproves claimed model physics success; Spotlight 5/9 motion clip recall and chance-like peak localization; learned visual/video judge and natural stutter ground truth still unverified. |

**Do not promote** an attractive general image/video model to autonomous acceptance because it describes the scene, recognizes a forklift, finds contours or emits confident structured JSON. The lookup registry should remain task-specific and distinguish *model availability*, *evidence collection*, *pass on a narrow checked property*, *inconclusive*, and *not qualified*. Selection and test sets are consumed by this research; later threshold fitting or model choice would require new independent source prompts, industrial assets, apps, user controls and time-coded video faults. In a real GUI project, defects and user feedback must be resolved at the source and retested in the running interface, not turned into debug prose on its front screen.
