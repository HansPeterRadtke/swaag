# Perceptual analyzer research — larger real-asset geometry and paired OCR comparisons

**Date:** 25 October 2026. **Owning project:** SWAAG, branch `research/visual-perception-20261024`. **Result:** useful new independently sourced negative evidence and one narrow positive signal, **not** a reliable general-purpose GUI, diagram, mechanical, or video QA gate. The complete source-linked aggregate numbers, hashes, subgroup results and grouped uncertainty are in `perceptual-asset-and-text-holdout-uncertainty-2026-10-25.json`. Original third-party assets, images and raw predictions remain exclusively under `/data/var/perceptual-independent-20261024/`; code and aggregate statistics alone enter Git.

## Why these experiments, not more demonstration fixtures

The owner's original `general/testing/Recording 2026-09-23 08-47-18-577.txt`, earlier GUI recordings and media-editing originals ask **which analyzers actually judge real rendered output with defensible false positives and misses**. Prior established work in this same chat already ruled out several weak claims: the Thor Qwen3-VL 8B clipped-text acceptance failure, trivially correct metadata fixtures, too many OCR/SSIM false alarms, ScreenVLM's slow token-constrained small-crop behavior, and five large VLMs' unreliable recognition of missing parts in human-annotated generated 3D objects. Browser `axe-core` plus DOM geometry is useful for the narrow relevant WCAG/layout properties, but not a universal visible-quality oracle. The previously published ScreenParser-YOLO 32-real-website evaluation found only 11/32 source-target box overlaps at IoU ≥0.50, despite thousands of correctly formatted UI element proposals; parsers **locate** UI pixels and cannot determine that a layout is *right*. This continuation focused on two still-open substantive questions: can frozen topology checks find *missing semantic mechanical parts* beyond the prior ten-asset toy pilot, and can *paired OCR text loss* improve detection of genuine typography/missing-content annotations over naive pixel-difference similarity?

## Original industrial GLBs: 129 acquired, 119 scored without retuning previous checks

**Independent asset source:** [3D-DefectBench original human-rated golden GLBs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zzhao0500/3D-DefectBench), pinned at its recorded model-dataset revision, CC BY-NC 4.0, noncommercial research. All **129** original GLB assets were retrieved and byte-size/header/SHA-256 verified, approximately **344.1 MB**. The original published `golden_labels.csv` provides two-rater majority decisions for form/surface, incomplete/fused objects, pose, extra parts and missing intended parts. The **ten** deliberately selected machinery-like assets from the earlier prototype were excluded from the new evaluation. The remaining **119 GLBs** were then analyzed using **exactly the previous frozen thresholds**, without reference to their human labels during geometry extraction: nonfinite vertex coordinates, any open/nonwatertight submesh, degenerate triangle face area, and rough world-space axis-aligned submesh box overlap exceeding **2%** of the smaller AABB volume. This test uses `trimesh` in an isolated CPU-only environment, not screenshots or rendering; original meshes are read-only and intact. The mesh predictions were checkpointed before joining human labels.

The 119-asset evaluation set contains **33 human-majority missing-parts positives and 86 negatives**. Compared with the human semantic question *"Is a requested component missing?"*, the unchanged geometry rules behaved as follows:

| Frozen numerical source-geometry flag | Missing-parts found (33) | Flagged no-missing objects (86) | Result for semantic missing-part task |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Any nonfinite vertex coordinates | 0 | 0 | Cannot detect a semantically absent but valid component |
| Any open/nonwatertight submesh | 33 | **86** | Rejects every asset, even every missing-parts negative |
| Degenerate triangular faces (preset relative tolerance) | 0 | 0 | No observed discrimination |
| Any rough AABB overlap over 2% of smaller volume | **9** | **20** | Recall **27.3%**, specificity **76.7%**, precision **31.0%** |

The raw continuous AABB overlap score achieved **ROC AUC 0.5233** for missing-parts labels. Across **2,000 prompt-grouped bootstrap replicates**, this was **0.435–0.624** (95% descriptive interval, not a proof of deployment distribution). The fixed AABB rule's recall interval was **11.5–45.8%**, specificity **65.1–86.5%**, precision **14.8–47.6%**. The ROC interval includes **0.5**, the no-information ranking benchmark. For *fused/incomplete* and *surface/form* gold questions, its source-overlap AUCs were only **0.604** and **0.573**. These are not industrial physics collision metrics; an overlap of *bounding boxes* says little about actual triangle intersection, contact, part support, joints or constraints. Open meshes can be intentional render-only surfaces; being watertight is not a prerequisite for a plausible game/visual-production model. Correctly finding a zero nonfinite-coordinate rate does not detect an absent door or linkage. A mesh whose expected steering assembly was never generated can be geometrically perfectly valid. This is **confirmatory negative evidence**, not an indictment of valid narrow numerical checks.

**Label robustness, explicitly scoped:** of the 119 evaluation assets, **21 missing-parts positives** had original two-rater *agreement 0.5*, and **12 positives plus all 86 negatives** had unanimous **1.0** agreement. Restricting to the more confident positive cases, AABB found only **3/12**, still producing **20/86** flags on unanimous no-missing cases. The full source's separate `aieval2026/3d-defectbench` *adjudicated* labels were linked to this 129-asset golden source using exact unique `(prompt, normalized generator version)` keys. **97** assets are unambiguously matched and all **97/97** majority and adjudicated labels agree for missing, fused and surface categories. **Two expert prompt/generator keys are ambiguous** because original prompts "dragon" and "guitar" were reused for distinct assets; **one** expert record has no matching source key. Therefore an earlier provisional **98/99** crosswalk claim from overwriting dictionary collisions was **too broad and is superseded**. The 97/97 source-adjudication consistency is not independent human reproduction because the same original project underlies both label releases; it verifies identity and transcription on the unique crosswalk, not the physical truth of those classes.

**Reproduction:** `tests/research/defectbench_full_golden_source_20261025.py`, `defectbench_golden_holdout_geometry_eval_20261025.py`. ## Paired OCR on authentic mobile GUI implementation differences: 132 screens across 52 apps

**Independent source:** [Scry Design Diff Eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Scrymore/scry-design-diff-eval), **311** original reference-A versus implementation-B mobile interface image pairs from **52 apps**, with **557** human-tagged issue regions/labels. Rather than simply rerunning the prior screen-difference test, the new hypothesis asks whether words visible in a reference disappear in the implementation and whether this correlates with human **Typography** or **Missing Content** tags. Selected at most **three pair IDs per app** by a stable SHA-256 ordering, independent of tags: **132 pairs across all 52 apps**. Tesseract **5.3.4**, PSM 11, processed both actual images after a bounded ≤640×1152 resize, one nice-level CPU thread, fixed **40% OCR confidence** filtering and ≥2-character alphanumeric tokens. All OCR tokens, input SHA and loss/Jaccard/string-similarity features were saved **before** accessing human issue tags for scoring. Approximately **453 seconds** of CPU elapsed for 264 image reads. Source app screenshots and recovered text remain under `/data/var`, not Git.

The grouped split reused the prior Scry comparison's app-hash grouping: **35 calibration pairs** and **97 evaluation pairs from different app groups**. This is a meaningful application-disjoint test of the frozen OCR output, but **not a completely untouched super-test**: the overall Scry dataset and split had already been used in previous strategy work. The positive reference is explicitly an externally tagged **Typography OR Missing Content** issue, not necessarily a missing textual word; icons and larger absent widgets sometimes carry `Missing Content`, and a font problem may keep exactly the same words. The negative group lacks those two tags but is **not human-certified free from all visual defects or legitimate content changes**. Those restrictions prevent overclaiming a strict real-world defect false-positive rate.

Only calibration labels were used to select among three predeclared token-change scores and the 0.00–0.90 thresholds in 0.05 steps. The chosen rule was **"more than 30% of uniquely recognized reference words missing in implementation"**. Its calibration balanced accuracy was **0.82** on 35 pairs; performance dropped on the 97 different-app evaluation pairs:

| 97 app-disjoint evaluation screenshots | Value |
|---|---:|
| Human-tagged typography/missing-content positives | 28 |
| Other-tagged/no-text-tag comparison controls | 69 |
| Correctly flagged tagged positives | **21** |
| Missed positives | **7** |
| Flags on control category | **34** |
| Controls not flagged | **35** |
| Recall / specificity vs available tags | **75.0% / 50.7%** |
| Precision vs available tags | **38.2%** |
| ROC area of continuous OCR reference-word-loss signal | **0.6211** |
| ROC area of previous frozen SSIM comparison on same examples | **0.4720** |

Prompt? No: **app-grouped** bootstrapping, two thousand resamples, produced an OCR AUROC 95% descriptive interval **0.487–0.754**; the *paired* ROC-area difference versus SSIM was **+0.149**, with grouped interval **+0.027 to +0.282** on these consumed evaluation groups. Thus **paired OCR contains modest useful text-change information not found by purely structural similarity**, but high control-flagging, OCR-recognition noise, intentional wording/scale variation and incomplete human labels prevent it from being an automatic rejector. A human design contract must specify required full visible text, allowed truncation and intended interaction state, or an image-only OCR *change* is merely triage evidence. Calibration-to-evaluation degradation is additional evidence against relying on the apparent 0.82 development score.

## Narrow answer to the original owner, and unresolved true acceptance work

**Currently independently supported tool roles:** Actual DOM geometry (client/scroll/ancestor clipping), actual browser CSS colors and `axe-core` with explicit exceptions for *measurable* GUI properties; source-linked OCR as an incomplete evidence collector; source-anchored diagrams and chart data/topology plus separately verified labels and legibility; actual 3D mesh/Blender transforms, joints, collision checks and human industry reference geometry as narrow physical oracles; exact approved timeline, PTS and content-preserving reference checks for narrowly defined video timing. ScreenParser v2 can output realistic element boxes but its preceding 32-real-page CPU run averaged 63 seconds, and only 11/32 user-specified DOM target boxes matched at IoU ≥0.5—never a standalone clipping-quality gate. ScreenVLM v2's earlier four small crops used too short a 384-token generation cap, so it is not scientifically fair to call the full model a universal failure either. Five large historical VLMs and their majority voting schemes missed most expert-labelled 3D missing parts. Generic CLIP global/patch image anomaly detections were poor on genuine MVTec LOCO products in the prior study. The human-annotated Spotlight video pilot showed simple optical-flow peaks were close to a chance temporal baseline, and the apparent real-vs-AI video preference success was confounded by native FPS; neither established a physical-motion or final-stutter judge.

**Not yet answered:** a specialized GUI model with trustworthy false alarms on broad **human-certified visible** clipped/overlapping/low-contrast natural screenshots; correctly interpreting diagram *meaning*, not just printing some labels; a real *engineering-certified* source assembly/kinematics plus photorealism benchmark for the owner's specific warehouse/forklift parts (most public anomaly datasets concern inspection of manufactured objects rather than missing CAD constraints); and naturally occurring stutter/tear/freezes with exact source-frame/time truth and genuinely clean, matched-codec controls, followed by independent video-language model comparison when shared GPU model services are actually idle. [MVTec 3D-AD](https://www.mvtec.com/research-teaching/datasets/mvtec-3d-ad) and [MVTec LOCO](https://www.mvtec.com/research-teaching/datasets/mvtec-loco-ad) are stronger real industrial sources for *domain-specific* anomaly detectors, not automatic generated-mechanical plausibility. The [LIVE Mobile Video Quality Database](https://www.colorado.edu/lab/live/live-mobile-video-quality-database) supplies real reference video and explicit frame-freeze distortions, but source acquisition requires a form or authenticated Globus; it was **not downloaded** in this continuation. The previous user-directed seventy-two-video Spotlight run and the warehouse source-framehash run had specific blocked output-retrieval operations, and **no attempt was made to replay, reroute or claim those effects**.

**Completion integrity:** Full original owner source recordings and Infra testing/GUI/media guidelines were reviewed earlier in this exact conversation and followed here. The dedicated SWAAG research worktree and branch were clean before these edits; production trading/3D/voice/other-agent processes remain untouched. The input labels, raw metrics, thresholds, dates, source SHA-256 and negative cases are retained, and source content is never staged in Git. These experiments justify a narrower hybrid-tool recommendation, **not** a completed all-domain visual judge.
