## Already-running foreground Coder: verified inference-only alternative, NOT approved

A low-memory alternative was evaluated without starting any additional model: the already resident **Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B Q4** model at Thor's established foreground endpoint. The private evaluator called the exact installed gateway `classify_turn_completion` and `classify_interruption` methods with a local-only `ClassifierConfig` override in its **own** interpreter. It passed no tools, executed no SWAAG operations or Android sends, and did not change running model/gateway configurations.

The first, fixed critical six-case pilot passed **6/6** (about `0.30–0.75 s` per case), but that small result did **not** qualify it. An independent one-use phase excluded those six cases from the 35-case historical oracle and attempted the remaining 29. Of the **25** completed decisions, **20 passed** and **five failed**: four different requests hit the full **eight-second** timeout and returned `classifier_invalid`, and the acknowledgement **“correct”** was wrongly judged to interrupt the assistant. The other **21 valid classifier responses** contained **20 correct** decisions; these conditional numbers must not hide the timeouts. One further test request was recorded as started when the evaluator was deliberately stopped to protect the shared Coder service; the outcome of that request is unknown, and **must never be replayed**. The remaining original cases were unattempted. This source-backed observation disqualifies this existing model/configuration as a **reliable live classifier** in the measured workload, despite its promising initial response accuracy.

The new **30-case** English/German and turn-interruption holdout was written, frozen and hashed **before** any proposed additional model query. It has deliberately **not** been run on Coder because the existing safety and latency acceptance was already failed. Do not claim a 30-case holdout score, replace the existing classifier, or extrapolate six pilot successes into population-level accuracy. Source-pinned bounded evaluation code and its regression tests are in `scripts/demos/verify_existing_coder_voice_classifier.py`, `scripts/demos/fixtures/voice_classifier_existing_coder_holdout_20261123.json` and `tests/test_verify_existing_coder_voice_classifier.py`. The published version adds a two-consecutive-invalid-response **circuit breaker** so a future distinct, authorized evaluation cannot monopolize an already busy model. Previously attempted model requests have not been replayed or overwritten.

Private raw evidence is under `/data/var/swaag-benchmarks/owner-serial-000062-20261123/` on Thor: the completed six-case pilot JSON, the interrupted 29-case phase JSON, and `coder-classifier-partial-stop-provenance.json`. Exact source and fixed-oracle SHA-256 identifiers, case timings, completed versus started requests and the refusal to retry are recorded there. The process was terminated **only for its own unique evaluation PID**; the production Coder service retained its original process identity and remained active.

**Remaining user-relevant work:** a real validated turn classifier that fits existing broker limits, a coordinated and reversible release of the server-side unknown-speaker hold, separate true owner-speaker and recorded-owner-audio defenses, and actual Android microphone, playback and reconnect acceptance. Until then, automatic tool-using voice turns are not qualified. Full independent multi-step tool execution remains limited to the earlier recorded read-only demonstration that completed **only after a distinct corrective supervisor turn**; no new fully autonomous first-pass multi-tool voice execution is claimed here.

## November 23 follow-up: already-running Qwen3.8 Q8 turn classifier, accurate initial decisions but slow

A separate no-action experiment evaluated **the existing, already-running Thor Qwen3.8 27B Q8 strong-model endpoint** rather than loading the missing dedicated classifier or retesting the disqualified Coder backend. An isolated interpreter imported the **actual installed voice gateway's unchanged** `classify_turn_completion` and `classify_interruption` source, locally rebound only its private `ClassifierConfig`, and used the strict published oracle. The eight pilot case identities were fixed before any Qwen3.8 inference: unfinished English request, completed stop command, acknowledgement, unfinished German request, completed correction, the previously Coder-failed acknowledgement “correct”, completed German statement and explicit interruption. Every model request was tool-free and carried the source's JSON decision schema, with no SWAAG RPC, voice submission or TTS output.

All **eight of eight** source-backed pilot decisions were correct and valid. Actual observed decision times were **3.34 to 5.13 seconds**, with a reported ninety-fifth-percentile sample at **5.08 seconds**. This is far too slow to claim instant spoken turn-taking or low-latency interruption, and eight cases do not measure population classification accuracy or performance under heavy concurrent use. The predeclared complementary **twenty-seven** oracle cases and distinct **thirty-case** independent holdout were **not run**: this is an inference-quality and latency pilot, **not a passing classifier acceptance or deployment recommendation**. The previous dedicated 3B and existing Coder alternatives also remain disqualified for their separately observed errors.

The original pilot source and its one-use original result are preserved privately on Thor as `/data/var/swaag-benchmarks/owner-serial-000063-20261123/verify_existing_qwen38_voice_classifier.py` and `qwen38-pilot8-original-noaction-result.json`, with SHA-256-checked inputs and the exact case times. The published reusable source `scripts/demos/verify_existing_qwen38_voice_classifier.py` is slightly **newer** than the actual run: after witnessing latency, the runner was hardened to refuse all later classifier inference phases unless a matching fully successful eight-case pilot has a **provisional two-second maximum ninety-fifth-percentile latency**. That threshold is an engineering safety gate, **not a claim of owner-selected latency requirements or demonstrated real-time responsiveness**. It rejects the saved five-second pilot by design. Regression tests verify immutable case selection, no implicit model effects, excluded prior results, single-use receipts and stop-on-uncertainty. The earlier model calls were **not repeated** to satisfy the later source change.

The practical conclusion is unchanged: production has no qualified fast dedicated turn classifier. Do not route unknown speakers or automatic instructions to tools based on either the successful small Qwen3.8 pilot or the incomplete Android client. The guarded Jetson candidate is still only a private preview under the existing `NO_GO` release decision, and no shared Qwen service was restarted, replaced, or reconfigured.
